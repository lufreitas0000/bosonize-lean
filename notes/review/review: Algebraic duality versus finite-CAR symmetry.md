## 2. Formal algebra: the exact corrected duality

Let \\(\mathcal A\_{\mathrm{osc}}\\) be the unital complex star algebra generated by the finitely indexed but unbounded-degree oscillator operators, and let \\(\mathcal A\_{\mathrm{ch}}\\) be the algebra generated by the signed charge shifts and diagonal charge functions on the algebraic charge-lattice carrier.

Work in

\\[ \mathcal A=\mathcal A\_{\mathrm{ch}} \otimes\_{\mathbb C}\mathcal A\_{\mathrm{osc}}, \\]

with charge and oscillator factors commuting. The formal vertex carrier is \\(\mathcal A[[t]]\\), with \\(t^\*=t\\) and \\(t\\) fixed by the duality.

All statements concern algebraic operators or coefficientwise formal series, not evaluated analytic exponentials.

### 2.1 Oscillator automorphism

Define

\\[ D\_{\mathrm{osc}}(A\_{Rm})=-A\_{Rm}, \qquad D\_{\mathrm{osc}}(C\_{Rm})=-C\_{Rm}, \\]

while fixing \\(A\_{Lm},C\_{Lm}\\).

For the right generators,

\\[ [-A\_{Rm},-C\_{Rn}] =[A\_{Rm},C\_{Rn}] =m\delta\_{mn}I. \\]

Mixed commutators remain zero. Since each adjoint pair receives the same real sign,

\\[ D\_{\mathrm{osc}}(a^\*) =D\_{\mathrm{osc}}(a)^\*. \\]

The defining CCR relations are preserved. Consequently, the substitution induces a complex-linear star automorphism on the algebra presented by those relations, and

\\[ D\_{\mathrm{osc}}^2=\mathrm{id}. \\]

This is a statement about the uncompressed polynomial CCR algebra, not about a global matrix representation on finite CAR.

### 2.2 Parameter and dressed-field covariance

The hyperbolic condition gives

\\[ (c-s)(c+s)=c^2-s^2=1. \\]

Thus, with \\(g=(c-s)^2\\),

\\[ g(P^\*)=(c+s)^2=g(P)^{-1}. \\]

The Bogoliubov substitutions obey

\\[ D\_{\mathrm{osc}}\circ\beta_P = \beta\_{P^\*}\circ D\_{\mathrm{osc}}. \\]

For example,

\\[ \begin{aligned} D\_{\mathrm{osc}}\beta_P(C\_{Rm})  &=-cC\_{Rm}+sA\_{Lm}\\\  &=-\beta\_{P^\*}(C\_{Rm}), \end{aligned} \\]

and similarly

\\[ D\_{\mathrm{osc}}\beta_P(C\_{Lm}) =\beta\_{P^\*}(C\_{Lm}). \\]

The adjoint identities follow automatically. Therefore,

\\[ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} D X_D(P,x)&=-X_S(P^\*,x),\\\ D X_S(P,x)&=-X_D(P^\*,x). \end{aligned}} \\]

These identities are correct for the stated \\(Q_R,Q_L,X_D,X_S\\) definitions.

### 2.3 The Klein-factor sign obstruction

Now adopt exactly the prompt's simplified Klein convention.

For

\\[ r(N_R,N_L)=(N_R,-N_L), \qquad U_r|N\rangle=|rN\rangle, \\]

define \\(D\_{\mathrm{ch}}(a)=U_raU_r^{-1}\\).

Direct evaluation on source kets gives

\\[ \begin{aligned} D\_{\mathrm{ch}}(F_R)&=F_R,\\\ D\_{\mathrm{ch}}(F_L)&=F_L^\dagger,\\\ D\_{\mathrm{ch}}(N_R)&=N_R,\\\ D\_{\mathrm{ch}}(N_L)&=-N_L. \end{aligned} \\]

Crucially, the left Klein factor transforms with no additional minus sign.

Define the full signs of the two Klein words on their source kets:

\\[ \begin{aligned} K_D(x)|N\rangle  &=(-1)^{N_R}\zeta^{(N_L-N_R-1)x}    |N+e_R-e_L\rangle,\\\ K_S(x)|N\rangle  &=(-1)^{N_R}\zeta^{(N_R+N_L)x}    |N-e_R-e_L\rangle. \end{aligned} \\]

The sign in the first line comes from \\(F_L\\); the final \\(F_R^\dagger\\) contributes no sign. Likewise for the second line.

After charge reflection,

\\[ D\_{\mathrm{ch}}K_D(x)|N\rangle  = (-1)^{N_R} \zeta^{-(N_R+N_L+1)x} |N+e_R+e_L\rangle. \\]

But reversing the complete ordered SC word gives

\\[ K_S(x)^\dagger|N\rangle  = (-1)^{N_R+1} \zeta^{-(N_R+N_L+2)x} |N+e_R+e_L\rangle. \\]

Hence

\\[ \boxed{ D\_{\mathrm{ch}}K_D(x) =-\zeta^xK_S(x)^\dagger. } \\]

The analogous calculation yields

\\[ \boxed{ D\_{\mathrm{ch}}K_S(x) =-\zeta^xK_D(x)^\dagger. } \\]

Since the oscillator and charge factors commute and \\(X_J=X_J^\dagger\\),

\\[ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} D W_D(P,t,x)  &=-\zeta^xW_S(P^\*,t,x)^\dagger,\\\ D W_S(P,t,x)  &=-\zeta^xW_D(P^\*,t,x)^\dagger. \end{aligned}} \tag{1} \\]

These are the exact identities under the prompt's explicitly prescribed bare charge reflection.

The discrepancy occurs already at order \\(t^0\\); therefore no higher-order oscillator calculation can repair it.

### 2.4 Recovering the manuscript's positive sign

Define instead a phased charge reflection

\\[ \widetilde U_r|N_R,N_L\rangle  = (-1)^{N_L}|N_R,-N_L\rangle. \\]

It satisfies \\(\widetilde U_r^2=I\\), and its conjugation action gives

\\[ \widetilde D\_{\mathrm{ch}}(F_R)=F_R, \qquad \widetilde D\_{\mathrm{ch}}(F_L)=-F_L^\dagger. \\]

Consequently,

\\[ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} \widetilde D W_D(P,t,x)  &=\zeta^xW_S(P^\*,t,x)^\dagger,\\\ \widetilde D W_S(P,t,x)  &=\zeta^xW_D(P^\*,t,x)^\dagger. \end{aligned}} \tag{2} \\]

Equation (2) reproduces the manuscript's desired identities without changing either vertex definition. What changes is the implementing charge reflection.

Both versions preserve the star operation and are involutive. The choice between them is a Klein gauge choice, not an interaction-dependent physical effect.

In fact, the two conventions are related by a diagonal charge-basis gauge:

\\[ G|N\rangle=(-1)^{f(N)}|N\rangle, \\]

where

\\[ f(N)=hN_R+ \frac{N_R(N_R-1)}2+ \frac{N_L(N_L-1)}2. \\]

Conjugating the prompt's Klein factors by \\(G\\) gives precisely the phase convention of manuscript equation (20.106). In that gauge, the unphased reflection \\(U_r\\) induces the transformation \\(F_L\mapsto-F_L^\dagger\\).

Thus the manuscript formula is consistent with its own Klein factors, but it is not consistent with the simultaneously specified simplified factors and bare \\(U_r\\).

### 2.5 Involutivity and transport of the state family

Let \\(D_P:\mathcal A_P\to\mathcal A\_{P^\*}\\) denote either of the two consistently chosen duality maps above.

Because the charge and oscillator substitutions are involutive,

\\[ D\_{P^\*}\circ D_P=\mathrm{id}. \\]

This also holds on the vertex words. For example, in the bare convention the two factors of \\(-1\\), together with \\(\zeta^x\overline{\zeta^x}=1\\), restore the original operator after applying the duality twice.

The oscillator state obeys

\\[ \omega_P=\omega_0\circ\beta_P^{-1}. \\]

The polynomial vacuum is invariant under the right-oscillator parity substitution. Therefore,

\\[ \begin{aligned} \omega\_{P^\*}(D\_{\mathrm{osc}}a) &=\omega_0\\!\left(\beta\_{P^\*}^{-1}D\_{\mathrm{osc}}a\right)\\\ &=\omega_0\\!\left(D\_{\mathrm{osc}}\beta_P^{-1}a\right)\\\ &=\omega_P(a). \end{aligned} \\]

Adding the charge expectation gives the exact family identity

\\[ \boxed{ \omega\_{P^\*,rN}(Da)=\omega\_{P,N}(a). } \tag{3} \\]

It holds for either charge gauge because the diagonal phase multiplying a charge basis vector cancels in its vector expectation.

More generally, for any finite ordered word,

\\[ \boxed{ \omega\_{P,N}(a_1a_2\cdots a_n) = \omega\_{P^\*,rN}(Da_1\\,Da_2\cdots Da_n). } \tag{4} \\]

The order of the factors is unchanged. Equation (4) also extends coefficientwise to formal power series.

For clarity, write \\(J\in\\{D,S\\}\\), \\(\bar D=S\\), \\(\bar S=D\\), and \\(W_J^{(+)}=W_J\\), \\(W_J^{(-)}=W_J^\dagger\\). Then the bare-reflection law implies

\\[ \begin{aligned} &\omega\_{P,N}\left( \prod\_{j=1}^n W\_{J_j}^{(\epsilon_j)}(P,t,x_j) \right)\\\ &\quad = \left[\prod\_{j=1}^n (-\zeta^{\epsilon_j x_j})\right] \omega\_{P^\*,rN}\left( \prod\_{j=1}^n W\_{\bar J_j}^{(-\epsilon_j)}(P^\*,t,x_j) \right). \end{aligned} \tag{5} \\]

Products on both sides retain the displayed ordering. For the gauge-adjusted reflection, remove the factor \\((-1)^n\\).

This is stronger than comparing the scalar Gaussian exponents \\(g\\) and \\(g^{-1}\\): it transports the entire ordered algebraic correlator family, including charge-selection rules and zero-mode phases.

It does not identify a particular physical finite-CAR density matrix. It also does not imply that \\(\omega\_{P,N}\\) is itself invariant at fixed \\((P,N)\\).

## 3. A finite-CAR particle–hole transformation exists, but is a different map

There is an exact finite-dimensional construction that deserves to be separated from the formal oscillator duality.

Assume two ordered species \\(R\<L\\), with \\(L=2h\\) momentum orbitals per species, labelled

\\[ I_h=\\{-h+1,\ldots,h\\}. \\]

The full occupation Fock space has dimension \\(2^{2L}\\), and its CAR operators use the lexicographic species/momentum ordering specified in the question.

### 3.1 Particle–hole transformation with momentum reflection

Define the involutive momentum permutation

\\[ f(k)=1-k. \\]

This maps \\(I_h\\) onto itself and reverses its order.

Consider the transformation on the CAR generators

\\[ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} \mathscr U c\_{Rk}\mathscr U^\dagger&=c\_{Rk},\\\ \mathscr U c\_{Lk}\mathscr U^\dagger&=c\_{L,f(k)}^\dagger. \end{aligned}} \tag{6} \\]

It preserves the CAR, including mixed-species anticommutation, and is implementable by a unitary on the full finite Fock representation.

An explicit implementation can be constructed without assuming a bosonization dictionary.

First perform left-species occupation complementation. If the left orbitals have indices \\(j=1,\ldots,L\\), define

\\[ C_L|S_R,S_L\rangle = (-1)^{\sum\_{j\in S_L}(j-1)} |S_R,I_h\setminus S_L\rangle. \\]

The exponent is precisely the parity string required by the fixed occupation order.

A direct creation/annihilation calculation gives

\\[ C_Lc\_{Lk}C_L^{-1}=c\_{Lk}^\dagger, \qquad C_Lc\_{Rk}C_L^{-1}=c\_{Rk}. \\]

Let \\(\Gamma_L(f)\\) be the ordinary fermionic second quantization of the orbital permutation \\(f\\), including its permutation signs, and set

\\[ \boxed{\mathscr U=\Gamma_L(f)C_L.} \\]

This gives equation (6). The transformation is unitary, parity-preserving for even \\(L\\), and can be normalized as an involution. With the above conventions, \\(\mathscr U^2=I\\).

### 3.2 Occupations and charges

Its action on configurations is

\\[ \boxed{ (S_R,S_L)\longmapsto \left(S_R,\ f(I_h\setminus S_L)\right), } \tag{7} \\]

up to the explicitly determined fermionic sign.

It follows that

\\[ n_R'=n_R,\qquad n_L'=L-n_L. \\]

Since \\(N\_\nu=n\_\nu-h\\),

\\[ \boxed{ N_R'=N_R,\qquad N_L'=-N_L. } \tag{8} \\]

The finite physical charge reflection is therefore exact. The admissible interval \\([-h,h]\\) is stable under it, and

\\[ \binom{L}{h+N_L} = \binom{L}{h-N_L}. \\]

However, equation (8) alone does not determine the action on density oscillators or spatial fields.

### 3.3 Sea configurations and excitation energy

An important reason to include \\(f(k)=1-k\\) is that it maps the actual filled seas correctly.

Let

\\[ S_N=\\{k\in I_h:k\le N\\}. \\]

Then

\\[ f(I_h\setminus S_N)=S\_{-N}. \\]

With the explicit parity convention above,

\\[ \boxed{ \mathscr U|\Omega\_{N_R,N_L}\rangle = (-1)^{N_L} |\Omega\_{N_R,-N_L}\rangle. } \tag{9} \\]

To check the sign, let \\(n=h+N_L\\). Complementation contributes \\((-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\\), while reversing the order of the \\(L-n\\) occupied orbitals contributes \\((-1)^{(L-n)(L-n-1)/2}\\). Their product is \\((-1)^{N_L}\\) for even \\(L=2h\\).

Write \\(E\_{\mathrm{rel}}(S)\\) for the CH07 vacuum-subtracted integer momentum energy, and \\(N=|S|-h\\). Using

\\[ \sum\_{k\in I_h}k=h, \\]

one obtains

\\[ E\_{\mathrm{rel}}\bigl(f(I_h\setminus S)\bigr) = E\_{\mathrm{rel}}(S)-N. \\]

But

\\[ E\_{\mathrm{ground}}(-N) = \frac{N(N-1)}2 = E\_{\mathrm{ground}}(N)-N. \\]

Subtracting the sector ground energies yields

\\[ \boxed{ e\bigl(f(I_h\setminus S)\bigr)=e(S). } \tag{10} \\]

Thus this transformation preserves the actual total excitation budget, not merely an artificial oscillator cutoff.

For every admissible \\(N\\) and every \\(K\ge0\\),

\\[ \boxed{ \mathscr U B(N,K)=B(rN,K), \qquad \mathscr U P(N,K)\mathscr U^\dagger=P(rN,K). } \tag{11} \\]

No frozen-margin assumption is necessary for this occupation-level equivalence. Margins become necessary when the physical budget is identified with a polynomial oscillator partition basis or when canonical current commutators are used.

This establishes a genuine finite-dimensional charge/budget equivalence, stronger than a purely abstract charge-lattice relabelling.

### 3.4 Density operators: the decisive mismatch

For nonzero integer transfer \\(m\\), the repository defines the nonwrapping density current as a sum over valid momentum pairs,

\\[ \rho\_\nu(m) = \sum\_{\substack{k,k+m\in I_h}} c\_{\nu,k+m}^\dagger c\_{\nu,k}. \\]

Using (6),

\\[ \begin{aligned} \mathscr U\rho_L(m)\mathscr U^\dagger &= \sum_k c\_{L,f(k+m)}c\_{L,f(k)}^\dagger\\\ &= -\sum_k c\_{L,f(k)}^\dagger c\_{L,f(k+m)}\\\ &=-\rho_L(m), \qquad m\ne0. \end{aligned} \\]

At zero transfer,

\\[ \mathscr U\rho_L(0)\mathscr U^\dagger =LI-\rho_L(0). \\]

Meanwhile all right currents are fixed:

\\[ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} \mathscr U\rho_R(m)\mathscr U^\dagger&=\rho_R(m),\\\ \mathscr U\rho_L(m)\mathscr U^\dagger&=-\rho_L(m) \quad(m\ne0). \end{aligned}} \tag{12} \\]

By contrast, the formal map \\(D\_{\mathrm{osc}}\\) negates the right oscillators and fixes the left oscillators.

Therefore the natural finite-CAR particle–hole transformation is not the finite implementation of the proposed oscillator sign automorphism, assuming the oscillator generators are identified with their correspondingly labelled physical currents.

This is an algebraic mismatch, not an approximation caused by finite size.

### 3.5 A global no-go for the proposed current signs

The mismatch can be strengthened to a no-go result for an exact global implementation of the formal current signs.

Assume \\(L\ge4\\), the represented mode set contains \\(m=1,2\\), and a finite-CAR unitary \\(V\\) satisfies

\\[ VN_RV^\dagger=N_R, \qquad V\rho_R(1)V^\dagger=-\rho_R(1), \qquad V\rho_R(2)V^\dagger=-\rho_R(2). \\]

Consider the sector containing one right fermion. On this sector, the nonwrapping density currents are the partial one-particle shift matrices \\(T_m\\).

The finite-band geometry gives

\\[ T_1^2=T_2\ne0. \\]

A unitary conjugation fixing \\(N_R\\) must preserve this one-particle sector. Consequently,

\\[ \begin{aligned} VT_2V^\dagger &=VT_1^2V^\dagger\\\ &=(VT_1V^\dagger)^2\\\ &=(-T_1)^2\\\ &=T_2. \end{aligned} \\]

But the stipulated action requires \\(VT_2V^\dagger=-T_2\\). This would force \\(T_2=0\\), a contradiction.

Thus:

\\[ \boxed{ \text{There is no global finite-CAR unitary realizing the formal right-current sign reversal for both }m=1,2, } \tag{13} \\]

under the stated charge-preservation and current-identification hypotheses.

This no-go does not apply to an abstract polynomial oscillator representation, nor to suitably restricted low-energy budgets on which the one-particle relation used in the argument is not present as an identity between the corresponding compressed current operators.

## 4. Boundary twists and exact physical order-parameter exchange

The finite particle–hole map does exchange CDW and SC operators, but the spatial phase depends on the chosen boundary trivialization.

Let the left-species twist have one-step phase \\(r_L\\), with \\(|r_L|=1\\), and write

\\[ c\_{L,b}(x)= L^{-1/2}\sum\_{k\in I_h} r_L^x\zeta^{kx}c\_{Lk}. \\]

The integer coordinate \\(x\\) is used here because the twisted field is naturally defined on the covering lattice, with its holonomy retained.

Applying (6) and substituting \\(j=1-k\\),

\\[ \begin{aligned} \mathscr U c\_{L,b}(x)\mathscr U^\dagger &= L^{-1/2}\sum_k r_L^x\zeta^{kx} c\_{L,1-k}^\dagger\\\ &= \zeta^x L^{-1/2} \sum_j r_L^x\zeta^{-jx}c\_{Lj}^\dagger. \end{aligned} \\]

If the target twist has one-step phase \\(r_L'\\), then

\\[ \boxed{ \mathscr U c\_{L,b}(x)\mathscr U^\dagger =\lambda\_{b,b'}(x)c\_{L,b'}^\dagger(x), \quad \lambda\_{b,b'}(x)=(\zeta r_Lr_L')^x. } \tag{14} \\]

The target right-species twist is unchanged.

Three specializations matter.

| Twist convention                            | Phase \\(\lambda(x)\\) |
| ------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Target step \\(r_L'=r_L^{-1}\\)             | \\(\zeta^x\\)          |
| Identical source/target step \\(r_L'=r_L\\) | \\((\zeta r_L^2)^x\\)  |
| Target step \\(r_L'=\zeta^{-1}r_L^{-1}\\)   | \\(1\\)                |

The first and third target choices have the same inverse holonomy, since \\(\zeta^L=1\\), but different one-step trivializations.

In particular, at the project's centered antiperiodic choice

\\[ r_L=e^{-\pi i/L}, \\]

one has \\(\zeta r_L^2=1\\), so the physical transformation preserves this twist record and produces no extra position-dependent phase.

For the finite order parameters,

\\[ O_D^b(x)=c\_{R,b}^\dagger(x)c\_{L,b}(x), \qquad O_S^b(x)=c\_{R,b}(x)c\_{L,b}(x), \\]

the exact ambient operator identities are

\\[ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} \mathscr U O_D^b(x)\mathscr U^\dagger  &=-\lambda\_{b,b'}(x)\\,        O_S^{b'}(x)^\dagger,\\\ \mathscr U O_S^b(x)\mathscr U^\dagger  &=-\lambda\_{b,b'}(x)\\,        O_D^{b'}(x)^\dagger. \end{aligned}} \tag{15} \\]

The minus signs follow from the cross-species CAR:

\\[ c_R^\dagger c_L^\dagger =-c_L^\dagger c_R^\dagger, \qquad c_Rc_L^\dagger=-c_L^\dagger c_R. \\]

These identities are exact at finite \\(L\\). They require no interacting vacuum, bosonic representation, or continuum limit.

They also establish why the manuscript's \\(\zeta^x\\) cannot be assumed to be a gauge-independent physical phase. The phase depends on the transformation of the boundary step data.

For a finite density matrix \\(\varrho\\), define \\(\varrho'=\mathscr U\varrho\mathscr U^\dagger\\). For example, the complete ordered two-point relation is

\\[ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} \operatorname{Tr}\bigl[ \varrho\\,O_D^b(x)^\dagger O_D^b(y) \bigr] ={}& \overline{\lambda(x)}\lambda(y)\\\ &\times \operatorname{Tr}\bigl[ \varrho'\\,O_S^{b'}(x)O_S^{b'}(y)^\dagger \bigr]. \end{aligned}} \tag{16} \\]

This is an exact finite-CAR correlation identity, but it relates explicitly transported density matrices. It does not identify \\(\varrho'\\) with the abstract A08 state.

## 5. Hamiltonian covariance: what can be established

The finite-CAR particle–hole transformation has a useful, and somewhat stronger, consequence for the particular CH17 current Hamiltonian.

### 5.1 Quadratic current part

Use the displayed finite Hamiltonian

\\[ H\_{\mathrm{osc}}(v_1,v_2) = \frac{2\pi}{L}\sum\_{m=1}^{M} \left[ v_1\bigl(\rho_R(m)\rho_R(-m) +\rho_L(m)\rho_L(-m)\bigr) + v_2\bigl(\rho_R(m)\rho_L(m) +\rho_R(-m)\rho_L(-m)\bigr) \right]. \\]

The finite densities are the actual nonwrapping CAR bilinears.

Equation (12) immediately gives

\\[ \boxed{ \mathscr U H\_{\mathrm{osc}}(v_1,v_2) \mathscr U^\dagger = H\_{\mathrm{osc}}(v_1,-v_2). } \tag{17} \\]

This statement is exact even outside current-CCR margins. It uses the CAR construction directly, not a global bosonic CCR representation.

### 5.2 Zero-mode and chemical-potential terms

Let

\\[ a=\frac{2\pi v_F}{L}, \qquad v_1=v_F+\frac{g_4}{2\pi}, \qquad v_2=\frac{g_2}{2\pi}. \\]

The supplied CH17 zero-mode expression can be rewritten exactly as

\\[ \begin{aligned} E_0(v_1,v_2,\mu;N) ={}& \frac{\pi}{L} \left[ v_1(N_R^2+N_L^2)+2v_2N_RN_L \right]\\\ &+\left(\frac{\pi v_F}{L}-\mu\right)(N_R+N_L). \end{aligned} \tag{18} \\]

The quadratic part is covariant under \\(v_2\mapsto-v_2\\), \\(N_L\mapsto-N_L\\). The linear part is not.

Indeed,

\\[ \boxed{ E_0(v_1,v_2,\mu;rN) = E_0(v_1,-v_2,\mu;N) + \left(2\mu-\frac{2\pi v_F}{L}\right)N_L. } \tag{19} \\]

Combining (17)–(19), the corresponding full finite-current Hamiltonian satisfies

\\[ \boxed{ \mathscr U H(v_1,v_2,\mu)\mathscr U^\dagger = H(v_1,-v_2,\mu) + \left(2\mu-\frac{2\pi v_F}{L}\right)N_L. } \tag{20} \\]

In particular, the symmetric chemical-potential choice

\\[ \boxed{\mu=\frac{\pi v_F}{L}} \\]

removes the residual and yields exact unitary covariance on the full finite occupation Fock space.

This matches the symmetric zero-mode convention used in manuscript equation (20.118). The manuscript's Hamiltonian is, however, written on the separate formal oscillator–charge algebra; equation (20) is an independently derived finite-CAR statement for the specific current Hamiltonian.

The zero-mode residual is a charge-dependent scalar within each fixed sector, but it is not a single scalar on the direct sum of sectors. If bare particle numbers rather than sea-subtracted charges are used, the particle–hole transformation also displays background constants such as \\(-\mu L\\); these reorganize into the term \\(2\mu N_L\\) when \\(n_L=h+N_L\\) is used.

A general microscopic Hamiltonian containing additional terms is not automatically covariant. For example, arbitrary nonlinear dispersions, asymmetric interactions, or fixed boundary conditions may generate further operators under the transformation.

### 5.3 What finite Hamiltonian covariance implies for states

Suppose the Hamiltonians satisfy the exact covariance law, and let \\(\varrho\_{P,N}\\) be a chosen density matrix supported in \\(B(N,K)\\). Define

\\[ \varrho\_{P^\*,rN} = \mathscr U\varrho\_{P,N}\mathscr U^\dagger. \\]

Then, for any ordered physical operator word \\(A\\),

\\[ \boxed{ \operatorname{Tr}(\varrho\_{P,N}A) = \operatorname{Tr}\left( \varrho\_{P^\*,rN} \\,\mathscr U A\mathscr U^\dagger \right). } \tag{21} \\]

If \\(\varrho\_{P,N}\\) is a ground-state projection for an exactly covariant Hamiltonian, its image is a corresponding ground-state projection. In a degenerate ground space, selecting a particular pure state still requires a choice.

Nothing in this argument proves

\\[ \varrho\_{P,N}\quad\text{corresponds to}\quad\omega\_{P,N} \\]

through the finite bosonization dictionary.

That identification remains an independent operator/state comparison problem.

## 6. Typed budget transport and residual operators

Even when an exact finite unitary exists, independently compressing intermediate products does not preserve their multiplication laws.

Let \\(P_s,P_m,P_t\\) denote actual charge/energy-budget projections. For any operators \\(A,B\\),

\\[ P_tAP_mBP_s = P_tABP_s-P_tA(I-P_m)BP_s. \tag{22} \\]

Because the finite particle–hole transformation transports the budget projections exactly,

\\[ \begin{aligned} \mathscr U\bigl[P_tA(I-P_m)BP_s\bigr]\mathscr U^\dagger ={}&P\_{rt}(\mathscr UA\mathscr U^\dagger)\\\ &\cdot(I-P\_{rm}) (\mathscr UB\mathscr U^\dagger)P\_{rs}. \end{aligned} \tag{23} \\]

Thus discarded channels are themselves covariant under the finite particle–hole transformation.

There is an additional subtlety: taking the adjoint of a CDW/SC operator reverses the order of its two fermionic factors. Its natural intermediate charge sector therefore changes.

For example, after transforming CDW, let

\\[ A=c_R^\dagger(x),\qquad B=c_L^\dagger(x), \\]

and let \\(N'=rN\\). Introduce two distinct intermediate projections:

\\[ Q_L=P(N'+e_L,K_L),\qquad Q_R=P(N'+e_R,K_R). \\]

Because \\(\\{A,B\\}=0\\),

\\[ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} P_tAQ_LBP_s ={}&-P_tBQ_RAP_s\\\ &-P_tA(I-Q_L)BP_s\\\ &-P_tB(I-Q_R)AP_s. \end{aligned}} \tag{24} \\]

This is an exact identity. In particular, the two residual terms cannot be omitted merely because the uncompressed words satisfy \\(AB=-BA\\).

Equation (24) is a useful corrected target for the remaining Chapter 20 bridge: it provides a typed comparison between the two projected order-parameter orderings, with every discarded channel visible.

The CH20 nonzero discarded-channel witness supplied in the baseline confirms why a residual-free theorem cannot be assumed generally.

### 6.1 A restricted implementation of the formal oscillator duality

There is nevertheless a smaller construction that can realize the desired formal sign pattern on sufficiently controlled finite-budget spaces.

Under the Haldane completeness and orthogonality margins, write normalized partition vectors as

\\[ |p_R,p_L;N\rangle, \qquad E(p_R)+E(p_L)\le K. \\]

The exact physical particle–hole unitary acts on these states as

\\[ \mathscr U|p_R,p_L;N\rangle = (-1)^{N_L+\ell(p_L)} |p_R,p_L;rN\rangle, \tag{25} \\]

where \\(\ell(p_L)\\) counts the number of positive left-current factors used to construct the partition state.

The \\((-1)^{\ell(p_L)}\\) arises from the minus sign on each left density current in (12).

By contrast, the gauge-adjusted formal duality should act as

\\[ \boxed{ T\_{N,K}|p_R,p_L;N\rangle = (-1)^{N_L+\ell(p_R)} |p_R,p_L;rN\rangle. } \tag{26} \\]

Where the partition vectors form a complete orthogonal budget basis, equation (26) defines an exact unitary

\\[ T\_{N,K}:B(N,K)\longrightarrow B(rN,K). \\]

It is related to \\(\mathscr U\\) by an additional parity operation on the oscillator partition labels of both species.

This establishes a useful distinction:

- \\(\mathscr U\\) is a full finite-CAR particle–hole implementation with exact physical-field covariance.
- \\(T\_{N,K}\\) is a typed finite-budget realization of the formal oscillator/charge label transformation, using the partition basis.

The second map need not arise from a global CAR automorphism with the prescribed generator action. In fact, the no-go in (13) excludes that stronger interpretation when its hypotheses apply.

Furthermore, equation (26) by itself is not a proof that dressed interacting finite-CAR vacua transform into one another. The previously established compressed dressed-annihilator counterexample remains relevant.

## 9. Remaining questions

The following choices must be settled before promoting a precise Chapter 20.3 or CH20b theorem statement.

3\. Meaning of finite duality: Is the intended target a physical CAR field transformation, a duality of the specified finite current Hamiltonian, or a typed isometry between low-energy partition carriers? These are different assertions and have different obstructions.

4\. Hamiltonian family: Is the symmetric chemical-potential convention \\(\mu=\pi v_F/L\\) part of the proposed physical theorem, or must the \\(N_L\\)-dependent residual in (20) be retained?

5\. Physical/vertex comparison: Does the manuscript seek full operator equality, ground-vector equality, equality of particular ordered correlators, or a norm bound on a difference? Each requires a distinct statement of the necessary budgets and intermediate channels.

None of these questions prevents the formal duality or the finite particle–hole theorem from being stated now. They determine how much stronger the connecting theorem can legitimately be.
