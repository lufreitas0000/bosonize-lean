# Appendix A05 proposal: exponentials, Klein maps, and vertex-operator scope

## Exponential domains

A locally nilpotent derivative has a finite exponential on each polynomial: for p of degree d, only j≤d contribute to `exp(tD)p`. Multiplication by X is not locally nilpotent on the polynomial ring; `exp(tX)1` is not a polynomial for nonzero t. Step-two nilpotency of the Lie algebra generated by D, X, I does not imply nilpotency of D or X as operators, nor existence of their exponentials on the chosen carrier.

The cross-mode version of chapter 8.16 also needs the Kronecker factor: its scalar exponent is $\alpha\beta\delta_{m,m'}$, not always αβ. For different modes the operators commute and there is no contraction factor.

Two legitimate choices are:

- Formal power series in an auxiliary parameter t, with identities interpreted coefficient by coefficient. These are formal algebraic identities and require no analytic limit or convergence evaluation. They may nevertheless enlarge the permitted carrier and should be explicitly approved.
- Finite expressions modulo t^(d+1), proving identities only through coefficient degree d. Every expression is finite, and the error/remainder is explicit.

**Definition 1 (Nilpotent Exponential):**
For a truly nilpotent endomorphism A with A^(d+1)=0 on its actual carrier, define $\operatorname{expNil}(A)=\sum_{j=0}^d A^j/j!$.

*Lean 4 Proof Strategy:*
Define the finite polynomial sum with scalar action `((j! : ℂ)⁻¹) • A^j`, parameterized by d. Prove power vanishing on the actual carrier before treating it as an exact nilpotent exponential. A ℚ-algebra or characteristic-zero field supplies the invertible factorials; do not divide in `Module.End`.

**Lemma 1 (Properties of Nilpotent Exponentials):**
Prove independence of a larger cutoff, its inverse expNil(−A), and the needed finite coefficient identities. Do not name an arbitrary Taylor polynomial the exact exponential of an unprojected operator.

*Lean 4 Proof Strategy:*

1. **Cutoff independence:** State `∀ d' ≥ d, expNil d' A = expNil d A` given `A^(d+1) = 0`. Prove this by splitting `Finset.range (d'+1)` into `range (d+1)` and the remainder, and show each term in the remainder is zero since `A^j = 0` for `j > d`.
2. **Inverse:** State `expNil d A * expNil d (-A) = 1` as endomorphisms. Prove via Cauchy product of finite sums. Use the binomial theorem on `(A - A)^k` to show all cross terms for `k > 0` cancel. Nilpotency guarantees the exact truncation without remainders.
3. **Auxiliary Lemmas:** `Commute A (-A)`, `Nat.factorial` properties, and a lemma relating the Cauchy product of finite sums of nilpotent commuting operators to the sum of `(X+Y)^n / n!`.

Compressed polynomial creators are nilpotent, but their commutator with derivatives has a boundary term and is not central. Therefore their finite exponentials do not inherit the scalar BCH relation from the ambient polynomial representation. For Wick/Hermite identities, direct word induction avoids this problem.

The chapter 8 text's claim that a Heisenberg Lie algebra has no nontrivial finite-dimensional representations is too strong. It has nontrivial finite-dimensional representations with a central element acting noninvertibly or by zero. The proved no-go is for a commutator equal to a nonzero scalar identity in characteristic zero. Infinite-dimensional polynomial endomorphisms present no trace paradox: a trace with cyclicity is not defined for these operators in the first place.

## Klein maps between admissible sectors

The Haldane basis is constructed only in the proved low-energy regime, not as a basis of the entire finite Fock space.

**Definition 2 (Klein Map):**
Define a Klein map between admissible source and target sector budgets of equal excitation cutoff, with the same partition-label set.

*Lean 4 Proof Strategy:*
Formalize the "source sector" and "target sector" as specific `Submodule`s of the full Fock space, parameterized by the excitation cutoff. The "partition-label set" serves as an indexing type for the basis of these submodules (`Module.Basis ι R M`). Define the Klein map as a `LinearEquiv` between these two submodules by defining it pointwise on the basis vectors indexed by the partition labels.

To use completeness in both sectors, require the multispecies completeness condition across all species: $\forall \eta \in \mathcal{C}, 2K + \max(|N_\eta|, |N_\eta - \delta_{\nu\eta}|) \le h$. $N_{\max}<h$ alone does not guarantee these equal partition dimensions for arbitrarily high K.

**Lemma 2 (Isometry of the Klein Map):**
Keep the normalized current Gram form from A02. Under the multispecies completeness condition $\forall \eta, 2K + \max(|N_\eta|, |N_\eta - \delta_{\nu\eta}|) \le h$, equal Gram factors in both sectors prove isometry of the map $F_{\nu, \vec{N}, K}: B(\vec{N}, K) \to B(\vec{N}-e_\nu, K)$. Define its inverse on the target budget; if a global ambient extension is desired, specify how the complementary subspace is handled and do not infer global commutation from the budget construction.

*Lean 4 Proof Strategy:*

1. **Isometry:** Equip the sectors with a Hermitian inner product on each finite carrier. Prove that for any basis elements `e_i, e_j`, `⟪KleinMap e_i, KleinMap e_j⟫_target = ⟪e_i, e_j⟫_source`. Since the Gram factors are identical by hypothesis, the `LinearEquiv` extends to a linear isometry.
2. **Inverse:** The inverse is intrinsically provided by `LinearEquiv.symm`.
3. **Ambient extension:** Specify compatible source and target complement maps after proving the orthogonal decompositions. Extension by zero is not unitary, and identity on different charge-sector complements is not automatically a typed map. Budget surjectivity comes from complete matching basis labels, not from an unspecified ambient extension.

Integer charge signs use integer powers, or an explicit parity character ℤ→{±1}, avoiding natural subtraction. The CAR lexicographic sign uses actual preceding occupation $\sum_{\eta<\nu}(h+N_\eta)$. The notes use relative charge only. The omitted constant $h\,\#\{\eta<\nu\}$ can be absorbed into chosen species phases, but that convention must be proved compatible with the fixed occupation-basis ground states. There can also be a same-species ground-ket phase from annihilating the top occupied mode.

Klein shifts have F² generally nonzero and do not generate a finite-dimensional Clifford algebra merely from cross-species anticommutation. Reserve Clifford terminology for separately constructed Majorana combinations with the relevant square relations.

## Vertex formula: a concrete obstruction

**Theorem 1 (Obstruction at K=0):**
Chapter 14's exact unprojected equivalence fails already at K=0. Take h=2, L=4, N=0 and x=0. The sea contains modes {−1,0}. The physical annihilator

$$
 \sqrt{L}\,c_x\Omega=c_{-1}\Omega+c_0\Omega
$$

has two nonzero orthogonal hole configurations, with relative-sector excitation energies 1 and 0. The proposed K=0 raising exponential is the identity, so FZ produces only the target sector ground ket. It cannot equal this physical c_xΩ. The R2 condition is satisfied. A saved exact CAR computation verifies the missing deep-hole component.

*Lean 4 Proof Strategy:*
Formalize this counterexample by `decide` or exact computation.

1. Define the specific finite-dimensional `Module` for `h=2, L=4, N=0`.
2. Define the vacuum state `Ω`, and operators $c_{-1},c_0,c_x$.
3. Evaluate $c_x\Omega$ and express it in the orthogonal hole basis.
4. Evaluate the proposed operator `FZ` at $K=0$.
5. Prove the inequality $\sqrt L\,c_x\Omega\ne FZ\Omega$ by showing their projections onto the excitation energy 1 subspace differ (one is non-zero, the other is zero).
This serves as a formal proof of negation (`¬ UniversalEquivalence`).

Even the nilpotency premise fails: W⁻Ω≠0 at K=0. Raising out of an input budget is not annihilation unless a projection is actually inserted. Finite full-Fock raising phases are nilpotent because the full energy spectrum has a finite top, but their nilpotency order is not K+1 for an input-only budget.

The zero-mode phase also conflicts with periodicity if written as a fractional or target exponent. To match the physical matrix element ${}_0\langle N-1|c_x|N\rangle_0 = L^{-1/2} (-1)^{h+N-1} \zeta^{xN}$, the zero mode $Z(x)$ must act on the source charge sector before $F$ shifts charge, so that the zero-mode part is $F Z(x)$ (or equivalently $Z_{\text{target}}(x) F$ with $Z_{\text{target}}$ having eigenvalue $\zeta^{x(N_{\text{target}}+1)}$). Placing an unshifted $Z$ after $F$ contributes $\zeta^{x(N-1)}$, differing by an extra $\zeta^{-x}$.

Furthermore $(W^+)^\dagger = -W^-$ and $(W^-)^\dagger = -W^+$. Consequently, adjoint exponentials carry minus signs and reverse their order:
$$
 (E_- E_+ F Z)^\dagger = Z^\dagger F^\dagger E_+^\dagger E_-^\dagger = Z^\dagger F^\dagger \operatorname{expNil}(-W^-) \operatorname{expNil}(-W^+).
$$
Placing $\operatorname{expNil}(-W^+)$ before $\operatorname{expNil}(-W^-)$ is an algebraic error because the two factors do not commute.

### Boundary Conditions, Zero Modes, and Phase Consistency with Chapter 5

In von Delft & Schoeller (vDS §2.2 and §3.2, Eqs. 2, 3, 5, 62–64), the continuum chiral fermionic field carries a boundary holonomy:
$$
 \psi(x + L) = \tau \psi(x), \qquad \tau = e^{i 2\pi \beta}.
$$
Standard anti-periodic boundary conditions (APBC) correspond to $\beta = -1/2$, where $\tau = -1$.

### 1. Integer Charge and Fixed-Holonomy Phase Independence
For integer charge $N \in \mathbb{Z}$, $e^{i 2\pi N} = 1$ identically. Consequently:
$$
 e^{i 2\pi (N + \beta)} = e^{i 2\pi \beta} = \tau.
$$
The spatial boundary phase of a fixed-flux fermion field is independent of $N$. A common misconception conflates this with the Jordan–Wigner transformation in periodic spin chains (e.g. the $XY$ model), where the fermion boundary term acquires an explicit particle-number parity factor $-(-1)^N$:
$$
 c_{L} = -(-1)^N c_0.
$$
That parity-dependent boundary condition arises from the Jordan–Wigner non-local string running across the seam, not from an integer-charge exponential phase $\zeta^{L N}$. Any parity-dependent spin-chain boundary condition requires its own model contract (Chapters 20 & 21).

### 2. Phase Consistency: The Field Fourier Phase and Vertex Phase
In `bosonize-lean`, the physical position fermion $c_x$ is already fixed in Chapter 5 (`Bosonize.Core.Ch05Fermions`) via the discrete Fourier transform on the periodic quotient lattice $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}/L\mathbb{Z}$:
$$
 c_x = a \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} \zeta^{k x} c_k, \qquad c_{x + L} = c_x \quad (\tau = 1).
$$
Consequently, the physical comparison field and the reconstructed vertex operator in Chapter 14 **must use the exact same phase parameter**:

- In the existing periodic baseline ($\tau = 1$), the zero mode must use the matching periodic formula:
  $$
   Z_\nu(x) |\vec{N}\rangle := \zeta^{x N_\nu} |\vec{N}\rangle, \qquad Z_\nu(x + L) = Z_\nu(x).
  $$
  Inserting an uncoordinated twist $\beta$ into $Z_\nu$ alone while leaving the Chapter 5 field periodic creates an immediate discrepancy.
- **Intertwining with the Klein Lowering Map:** The primary algebraic role of $Z_\nu(x)$ is to supply the source charge eigenvalue to the ground-to-ground CAR matrix element before $F_\nu$ lowers charge:
  $$
   F_\nu Z_\nu(x) |\vec{N}\rangle_0 = P(\nu, \vec{N}) \zeta^{x N_\nu} |\vec{N} - e_\nu\rangle_0 = \sqrt{L} \, {}_0\langle \vec{N} - e_\nu | c_{(\nu, x)} | \vec{N}\rangle_0.
  $$
  Placing $Z_\nu$ after $F_\nu$ introduces an extra position-dependent factor $\zeta^{-x}$, but does not change the boundary holonomy.

### 3. Additive Implementation Contract for General Holonomy $\tau$
To rigorously support arbitrary spatial twists $\tau$ without modifying the frozen Core sources:

1. **Integer-Lift Field Formulation:**
   Choose a unit complex number $r \in \mathbb{C}$ ($|r| = 1$) such that $r^L = \tau$. Define the integer-lifted field on $n \in \mathbb{Z}$:
   $$
    c_r(n) := r^n \cdot c_0([n]), \qquad c_r(n + L) = r^L c_r(n) = \tau c_r(n)
   $$
   where $[n] = n \bmod L \in \mathbb{Z}/L\mathbb{Z}$, and $c_0([n])$ is the Chapter 5 periodic field.
2. **Matching Zero Mode:**
   In Chapter 14, the matching source zero-mode operator is:
   $$
    Z_r(n) |\vec{N}\rangle := r^n \zeta^{n N} |\vec{N}\rangle, \qquad Z_r(n + L) = \tau Z_r(n).
   $$
3. **Budget and Energy Bridge (A03/CH07):**
   For linear chiral dispersion with momentum offset $\beta$, shifting one-particle labels $k \mapsto k + \beta$ gives:
   $$
    P_\beta(S) = P_0(S) + \beta N(S), \qquad t_\beta(N) = t_0(N) + \beta N, \qquad e_\beta(S) = P_\beta(S) - t_\beta(N(S)) = e_0(S).
   $$
   Because excitation energies $e_\beta(S) = e_0(S)$ are strictly invariant under this uniform linear shift, the excitation budgets and partition counting of A03/CH07 are fully reusable, requiring only a rational/real scalar bridge for the physical sector ground energy $t_\beta(N) = N^2/2$ at centered APBC $\beta = -1/2$.
