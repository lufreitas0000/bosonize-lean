\chapter*{Notation and Conventions}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Notation and Conventions}
\markboth{Notation and Conventions}{Notation and Conventions}

\begingroup\small
This page records the notation used repeatedly in the book. Chapter-specific
hypotheses and conventions are stated where they are needed.

\begin{itemize}
    \setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
    \setlength{\topsep}{3pt}
    \item \textbf{Lattice and momentum band.} The periodic lattice is
    $\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}/L\mathbb{Z}$, the quotient of integer site labels by
    translations through $L$ sites. When $L=2h$, its centered momentum band is
    $\Lambda^*=\{-h+1,\ldots,h\}$, retaining the positive Nyquist mode.
    The root of unity $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/L}$ is a complex scalar.

    \item \textbf{Fourier character and differences.} For $k\in\Lambda^*$ and
    $x\in\Lambda$, the character $\chi(k,x)=\zeta^{kx}$ uses the canonical
    residue of $x$ in the exponent. On functions $f:\Lambda\to\mathbb C$,
    translation is the map $E f(x)=f(x+1)$ and the forward difference is the
    endomorphism $\Delta=E-I$ of that function space.

    \item \textbf{Fermions and the reference sea.} Momentum operators
    $c_k,c_k^\dagger$ act on the fermionic Fock space generated by the finite
    set of modes $k\in\Lambda^*$; spatial operators $\psi_x,\psi_x^\dagger$
    act on the same Fock space for $x\in\Lambda$.
    The half-filled sea is $|\Omega\rangle$, with occupied modes
    $\mathcal{S}_0=\{-h+1,\ldots,0\}$. A charge sector is indexed by
    $N=\#S-h$, and is admissible when $-h\le N\le h$.

    \item \textbf{Energy and finite budgets.} The excitation energy above the
    ground state in sector $N$ is $e(S)$; $B(N,K)$ denotes the subspace of
    the fermionic Fock space spanned by states in that sector with $e(S)\le K$.
    Any identity restricted to $B(N,K)$
    carries the margin hypotheses stated with that result.

    \item \textbf{Density modes.} The non-wrapping density mode of integer
    transfer $m$ is $\rho_m$, acting on the fermionic Fock space with its
    domain restrictions specified where it is used. Normal ordering is written
    $:\!\rho_m\!:$ .
    Current commutators in this book hold on their stated finite budgets;
    they are not asserted globally on the full finite-dimensional Fock space.

    \item \textbf{Chirality and species.} The labels $R$ and $L$ denote the
    right- and left-moving branches, with species labels $\nu_R$ and
    $\nu_L$. A species charge is $N_\nu\in\mathbb Z$, and
    $\vec N=(N_\nu)_\nu$ records all charges. The unit vector $e_\nu$
    changes only the charge of species $\nu$.

    \item \textbf{Joint budgets and mode cutoffs.} For several species,
    $B(\vec N,K)$ restricts the charge vector to $\vec N$ and the \emph{sum}
    of their excitation energies to at most $K$. The integer $M$ is a
    positive density-mode cutoff; margin conditions accompanying a theorem
    specify how far the states and intermediate operations remain from the
    finite-band edges.

    \item \textbf{Chiral and dual fields.} The finite chiral fluctuation
    fields are $\varphi_R,\varphi_L$; the phase and dual phase fields are
    $\phi=\varphi_R+\varphi_L$ and $\theta=\varphi_R-\varphi_L$. Their
    commutator identities are stated on the energy budgets where they hold.
    The spatially uniform ($m=0$) charge degree of freedom is treated
    separately from these nonzero-mode fluctuations.

    \item \textbf{Boundary holonomy.} A twist $\tau_\nu$ is the phase
    acquired by species $\nu$ after one circuit of the ring;
    $\psi_\nu(x+L)=\tau_\nu\psi_\nu(x)$. A chosen one-step phase $r_\nu$
    satisfies $r_\nu^L=\tau_\nu$.

    \item \textbf{Bosonic polynomial model.} For a positive mode cutoff $M$,
    the polynomial carrier is $\mathcal{F}_b=\mathbb{C}[X_1,\ldots,X_M]$.
    The creation map $C_m:\mathcal{F}_b\to\mathcal{F}_b$ is multiplication
    $C_m p=X_m p$ and the annihilation map $A_m:\mathcal{F}_b\to\mathcal{F}_b$
    is $A_m p=m\,\partial p/\partial X_m$. Its weighted degree is
    $E(r)=\sum_{m=1}^M m r_m$. In later chapters $A_m,C_m$ also denote the
    oscillator annihilation and creation maps; $\omega_0,\omega_P$ are
    algebraic state functionals, not finite-Fock vectors or traces.
\end{itemize}

We use Dirac notation with inner products linear in the ket:
$\langle u|A|v\rangle=\langle u,Av\rangle$. An endomorphism
$A\in\operatorname{End}(V)$ is a linear map from a space $V$ to itself;
operators are italicized, and operator products denote composition, with the
rightmost operator acting first. A displayed theorem states its own carrier
and assumptions.
\endgroup
