\chapter{Position and Momentum Fermions}
\label{ch:fermions}

\section{Introduction and Physical Framework}

Having established the abstract Canonical Anticommutation Relations (CAR) and Fock space representations in Chapter~\ref{ch:car_fock}, and the discrete Fourier transform in Chapter~\ref{ch:fourier}, we now unite these structures to describe physical fermions on the one-dimensional lattice.

Throughout this chapter and the remainder of the monograph, the lattice length is assumed to be an even positive integer:
\begin{equation}
L = 2h, \quad h \in \mathbb{N}, \quad h \ge 1.
\end{equation}
The single-particle index set $\iota$ is instantiated as the centered momentum band:
\begin{equation}
\iota = \Lambda^* = \left\{ k \in \mathbb{Z} \;\middle|\; -L < 2k \le L \right\} = \{-h+1, -h+2, \dots, h-1, h\},
\end{equation}
endowed with the standard linear order inherited from $\mathbb{Z}$. 

The fermionic state space is the Fock space over the momentum band:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{F}(L) \coloneqq \ell^2(\mathcal{P}(\Lambda^*)) \equiv \bigoplus_{S \subseteq \Lambda^*} \mathbb{C} |S\rangle,
\end{equation}
which has exact complex dimension $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} \mathcal{F}(L) = 2^L$. The momentum creation and annihilation operators $\{c_k, c_k^\dagger \mid k \in \Lambda^*\}$ satisfy the standard CAR algebra:
\begin{equation}
\{c_k, c_p^\dagger\} = \delta_{k, p} I, \qquad \{c_k, c_p\} = 0, \qquad \{c_k^\dagger, c_p^\dagger\} = 0, \qquad c_k^\dagger = (c_k)^\dagger.
\end{equation}
All momentum operators $c_k,c_k^\dagger$ are endomorphisms of the finite Fock space, $c_k,c_k^\dagger\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(\mathcal F(L))$. The symbols $c_k,c_k^\dagger$ are reserved strictly for momentum modes.

\section{Spatial Fermions via the Unitary Fourier Transform}

In continuous quantum field theory, field operators $\psi(x)$ are operator-valued distributions that must be smeared with test functions. On our discrete spatial circle $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}/L\mathbb{Z}$, spatial fermions are well-defined bounded operators constructed as finite linear combinations of momentum modes.

Recall from Chapter~\ref{ch:fourier} the real normalization factor $a \coloneqq 1/\sqrt{L} \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}$ satisfying $L a^2 = 1$, and the canonical primitive $L$-th root $\zeta = e^{2\pi i / L}$.

\begin{definition}[Spatial Fermionic Operators]
For every spatial site $x \in \Lambda$, the spatial annihilation operator $\psi_x$ and spatial creation operator $\psi_x^\dagger$ are defined by:
\begin{align}
\psi_x &\coloneqq \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} \chi(k, x)\, c_k = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} \zeta^{k x}\, c_k, \label{eq:spatial_psi} \\
\psi_x^\dagger &\coloneqq \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} \chi(-k, x)\, c_k^\dagger = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} \zeta^{-k x}\, c_k^\dagger. \label{eq:spatial_psidag}
\end{align}
For each $x\in\Lambda$, the operators $\psi_x,\psi_x^\dagger,n_x$ act on $\mathcal F(L)$ and belong to $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(\mathcal F(L))$. The local spatial fermion number operator is defined by:
\begin{equation}
n_x \coloneqq \psi_x^\dagger \psi_x.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Hilbert Adjoint Compatibility]
The spatial creation operator is the exact Hilbert adjoint of the spatial annihilation operator:
\begin{equation}
(\psi_x)^\dagger = \psi_x^\dagger \quad \text{for all } x \in \Lambda.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $1/\sqrt L$ is real and the adjoint is conjugate-linear,
\begin{equation}
(\psi_x)^\dagger
=\left(\frac1{\sqrt L}\sum_{k\in\Lambda^*}\zeta^{kx}c_k\right)^\dagger
=\frac1{\sqrt L}\sum_{k\in\Lambda^*}\overline{\zeta^{kx}}\,c_k^\dagger
=\frac1{\sqrt L}\sum_{k\in\Lambda^*}\zeta^{-kx}c_k^\dagger
=\psi_x^\dagger,
\end{equation}
using $(c_k)^\dagger=c_k^\dagger$ and the definition~\eqref{eq:spatial_psidag}.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact Position CAR Inheritance}

The fundamental theorem of discrete spatial fermions is that they inherit the local canonical anticommutation relations without distortion.

\begin{theorem}[Exact Spatial CAR]
\label{thm:spatial_car}
For all spatial sites $x, y \in \Lambda$, the spatial operators satisfy:
\begin{align}
\{\psi_x, \psi_y^\dagger\} &= \delta_{x, y} I, \label{eq:pos_car_mixed} \\
\{\psi_x, \psi_y\} &= 0, \label{eq:pos_car_ann} \\
\{\psi_x^\dagger, \psi_y^\dagger\} &= 0. \label{eq:pos_car_cre}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the mixed anticommutator, the momentum CAR and dual character orthogonality give
\begin{align}
\{\psi_x,\psi_y^\dagger\}
&=\frac1L\sum_{k,p\in\Lambda^*}\chi(k,x)\chi(-p,y)\{c_k,c_p^\dagger\}
=\frac1L\sum_{k\in\Lambda^*}\zeta^{k(x-y)}I\\
&=\frac1L\bigl(L\delta_{x,y}\bigr)I=\delta_{x,y}I.
\end{align}
The two equal-type anticommutators vanish by $\{c_k,c_p\}=0$ and its creation counterpart, since each is a linear combination of the corresponding momentum anticommutators.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Formal Correctness vs.\ Physical Implications: Global Fourier vs.\ Jordan--Wigner]
Notice a remarkable conceptual feature of our construction: spatial fermions $\psi_x$ are obtained directly as global unitary Fourier transforms of momentum fermions $c_k$. In standard 1D lattice physics (such as Lieb, Schultz, and Mattis~\cite{lieb1961}), lattice fermions are frequently introduced through Jordan--Wigner transformations from Pauli spin matrices $\sigma_j^\pm$:
\begin{equation}
\psi_j = \left( \prod_{l < j} \sigma_l^z \right) \sigma_j^-.
\end{equation}
While physically intuitive for quantum spin chains, Jordan--Wigner strings introduce non-local phase tails that complicate spatial locality on periodic rings. In our formulation, the spatial operators $\psi_x$ anticommute locally across distinct sites $\{\psi_x, \psi_y^\dagger\} = \delta_{x, y} I$ as an exact consequence of character orthogonality, requiring no string operators whatsoever.
\end{remark}

\section{Operator Fourier Inversion}

The relationship between position and momentum fermions is completely symmetric under Fourier inversion.

\begin{theorem}[Fourier Inversion for Operators]
\label{thm:op_fourier_inversion}
The momentum operators are recovered from spatial fermions via the inverse discrete Fourier transform:
\begin{align}
c_k &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{x \in \Lambda} \chi(-k, x)\, \psi_x = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{x \in \Lambda} \zeta^{-k x}\, \psi_x, \label{eq:inv_ck} \\
c_k^\dagger &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{x \in \Lambda} \chi(k, x)\, \psi_x^\dagger = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{x \in \Lambda} \zeta^{k x}\, \psi_x^\dagger. \label{eq:inv_ckdag}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Substituting~\eqref{eq:spatial_psi} and using spatial character orthogonality,
\begin{equation}
\frac1{\sqrt L}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\chi(-k,x)\psi_x
=\frac1L\sum_{p\in\Lambda^*}\left[\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\chi(p-k,x)\right]c_p
=\frac1L\sum_{p\in\Lambda^*}(L\delta_{p,k})c_p=c_k.
\end{equation}
Taking Hilbert adjoints gives the identity for $c_k^\dagger$.
\end{proof}

\section{Conservation of Total Particle Number}

\begin{theorem}[Particle Number Invariance]
\label{thm:number_invariance}
The total particle number $N_{\mathrm{tot}}$ is an endomorphism of $\mathcal F(L)$, $N_{\mathrm{tot}}\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(\mathcal F(L))$, and satisfies:
\begin{equation}
N_{\mathrm{tot}} \coloneqq \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} c_k^\dagger c_k = \sum_{x \in \Lambda} \psi_x^\dagger \psi_x = \sum_{x \in \Lambda} n_x.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Substituting the spatial definitions and applying $\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\chi(p-k,x)=L\delta_{p,k}$,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\psi_x^\dagger\psi_x
=\frac1L\sum_{k,p\in\Lambda^*}c_k^\dagger c_p\left[\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\chi(p-k,x)\right]
=\sum_{k\in\Lambda^*}c_k^\dagger c_k=N_{\mathrm{tot}}.
\end{equation}
\end{proof}

\section{The Dirac Fermi Sea and Linearized Hamiltonians}

In relativistic field theory and 1D condensed matter physics, the physical vacuum is not the bare state $|\emptyset\rangle$ devoid of particles, but the filled Dirac sea where all negative-energy single-particle levels are occupied.

\begin{definition}[Dirac Sea Configuration and Vacuum]
For even lattice length $L = 2h$, the half-filled Dirac Fermi sea configuration $\mathcal{S}_0 \subset \Lambda^*$ consists of all non-positive momentum modes:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}_0 \coloneqq \{k \in \Lambda^* \mid k \le 0\} = \{-h+1, -h+2, \dots, -1, 0\}.
\end{equation}
The cardinality of the Fermi sea is $\#\mathcal{S}_0 = h = L/2$. The Dirac sea vacuum state is the corresponding occupation state:
\begin{equation}
|\Omega\rangle \coloneqq |\mathcal{S}_0\rangle \in \mathcal{F}(L).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Bare and Physical Momentum Hamiltonians]
The next operator measures the signed momentum of an occupation configuration relative to the chosen band. Here we study only its algebraic action at a fixed time; no time evolution or dynamical equation is assumed. Subtracting the sea energy then makes the reference Dirac sea have zero excitation energy.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The bare kinetic Hamiltonian $H_0\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(\mathcal F(L))$ is defined by:
    \begin{equation}
    H_0 \coloneqq \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} k\, c_k^\dagger c_k = \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} k\, n_k.
    \end{equation}
    \item The ground state sea energy $E_\Omega$ is the eigenvalue of $H_0$ on $|\Omega\rangle$:
    \begin{equation}
    E_\Omega \coloneqq \sum_{k \in \mathcal{S}_0} k.
    \end{equation}
    \item The physical, normal-ordered Hamiltonian $P\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(\mathcal F(L))$ is defined by:
    \begin{equation}
    P \coloneqq H_0 - E_\Omega \cdot I,
    \end{equation}
    so that $P |\Omega\rangle = 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Evaluation of Ground State Energy]
For $L = 2h$ with $h \ge 1$, the bare ground state energy evaluates to:
\begin{equation}
E_\Omega = -\frac{h(h-1)}{2}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By definition, $\mathcal{S}_0 = \{-h+1, \dots, 0\}$. Summing the consecutive negative integers:
\begin{equation}
E_\Omega = \sum_{j=-h+1}^0 j = - \sum_{m=1}^{h-1} m = - \frac{(h-1) h}{2} = -\frac{h(h-1)}{2}.
\end{equation}
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Spectra and Commutation Relations]
\label{thm:h0_commutators}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item On any occupation basis state $|S\rangle$ ($S \subseteq \Lambda^*$):
    \begin{equation}
    H_0 |S\rangle = \left( \sum_{k \in S} k \right) |S\rangle, \qquad P |S\rangle = \left( \sum_{k \in S} k - E_\Omega \right) |S\rangle.
    \end{equation}
    \item For any mode $p \in \Lambda^*$, the creation and annihilation operators satisfy:
    \begin{align}
    [H_0, c_p^\dagger] &= [P, c_p^\dagger] = p\, c_p^\dagger, \\
    [H_0, c_p] &= [P, c_p] = -p\, c_p.
    \end{align}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We prove each statement:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Each $n_k$ is diagonal on basis states: $n_k |S\rangle = |S\rangle$ if $k \in S$, and $0$ otherwise. Summing over $k \in \Lambda^*$ gives $H_0 |S\rangle = (\sum_{k \in S} k) |S\rangle$. Subtracting $E_\Omega I$ gives the eigenvalue for $P$.
    \item Using $H_0=\sum_{k\in\Lambda^*}k n_k$ and the bilinear CAR relations $[n_k,c_p^\dagger]=\delta_{k,p}c_p^\dagger$ and $[n_k,c_p]=-\delta_{k,p}c_p$ from Theorem~\ref{thm:algebraic_car}(2),
    \begin{align}
    [H_0,c_p^\dagger]&=\sum_{k\in\Lambda^*}k[n_k,c_p^\dagger]=p c_p^\dagger,\\
    [H_0,c_p]&=\sum_{k\in\Lambda^*}k[n_k,c_p]=-p c_p.
    \end{align}
    Since $P=H_0-E_\Omega I$ differs from $H_0$ by a scalar multiple of the identity, its commutators are the same.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Formal Correctness vs.\ Physical Implications: Linearization and the Dirac Sea]
In standard Luttinger liquid theory (e.g., Haldane~\cite{haldane1981}, Giamarchi~\cite{giamarchi2004}, and von Delft--Schoeller~\cite{vondelft1998}), the kinetic dispersion is linearized near the Fermi points $\pm k_F$. The spectrum is extended to $-\infty$, yielding an infinite Dirac sea. While this makes the model solvable, it leads to an infinite ground state energy $\sum_{k \le 0} k = -\infty$, requiring formal infinite normal ordering.

In our finite constructive formulation, the Dirac sea $\mathcal{S}_0$ has a finite bottom at mode $-h+1$. The ground state energy $E_\Omega = -h(h-1)/2$ is an exact finite integer. No infinite subtraction is required. At the same time, for low-energy excitations near the Fermi surface $k \approx 0$, the spectrum is strictly linear ($\epsilon_k = k$), exactly matching the physics of chiral 1D fermions.
\end{remark}

\section{Chapter Summary and Literature Comparison}

In this chapter, we united the Fourier transform and CAR representations to describe physical lattice fermions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Spatial fermionic operators $\psi_x, \psi_x^\dagger$ are constructed via the unitary Fourier transform, leaving $c_k, c_k^\dagger$ strictly reserved for momentum modes.
    \item Spatial operators satisfy exact local CAR relations $\{\psi_x, \psi_y^\dagger\} = \delta_{x, y} I$ without non-local Jordan--Wigner strings.
    \item Total particle number is strictly conserved across representations: $N_{\mathrm{tot}} = \sum_k c_k^\dagger c_k = \sum_x \psi_x^\dagger \psi_x$.
    \item The half-filled Dirac sea $\mathcal{S}_0 = \{-h+1, \dots, 0\}$ provides a finite ground state $|\Omega\rangle$ with exact finite energy $E_\Omega = -h(h-1)/2$.
    \item The physical momentum Hamiltonian $P = H_0 - E_\Omega I$ generates exact energy shifts $[P, c_p^\dagger] = p c_p^\dagger$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Comparison with Standard Continuum Literature.}
In standard physics texts, such as Giamarchi~\cite{giamarchi2004} and Gogolin et al.~\cite{gogolin1998}, spatial fermions $\psi(x)$ and momentum modes $c_k$ are introduced simultaneously with continuous spatial indices $x \in [0, \mathcal{L}]$. Field operators satisfy singular anticommutators $\{\psi(x), \psi^\dagger(y)\} = \delta(x-y)$. In contrast, our finite lattice fermions $\psi_x$ are bounded operators on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space of dimension $2^L$. All sums are finite, operator products are well-defined, and the Dirac sea is mathematically regular, setting the stage for the Algebraic Quantum Field Theory local net in Chapter~\ref{ch:lattice_aqft_net}.
