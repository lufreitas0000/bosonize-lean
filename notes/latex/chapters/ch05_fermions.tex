\chapter{Position and Momentum Fermions}
\label{ch:fermions}

\section{Introduction and Physical Framework}

Having established the abstract Canonical Anticommutation Relations (CAR) and Fock space representations in Chapter~\ref{ch:car_fock}, and the discrete Fourier transform in Chapter~\ref{ch:fourier}, we now unite these structures to describe physical fermions on the one-dimensional lattice.

Throughout this chapter and the remainder of the monograph, the lattice length is assumed to be an even positive integer:
\begin{equation}
L = 2h, \quad h \in \mathbb{N}, \quad h \ge 1.
\end{equation}
The single-particle index set $\iota$ is instantiated as the centered momentum band:
\begin{equation}
\iota = \Lambda^* = \left\{ k \in \mathbb{Z} \;\middle|\; -L < 2k \le L \right\} = \{-h+1, -h+2, \dots, h-1, h\},
\end{equation}
endowed with the standard linear order inherited from $\mathbb{Z}$. 

The fermionic state space is the Fock space over the momentum band:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{F}(L) \coloneqq \ell^2(\mathcal{P}(\Lambda^*)) \equiv \bigoplus_{S \subseteq \Lambda^*} \mathbb{C} |S\rangle,
\end{equation}
which has complex dimension $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} \mathcal{F}(L) = 2^L$. The momentum creation and annihilation operators $\{c_k, c_k^\dagger \mid k \in \Lambda^*\}$ satisfy the standard CAR algebra:
\begin{equation}
\{c_k, c_p^\dagger\} = \delta_{k, p} I, \qquad \{c_k, c_p\} = 0, \qquad \{c_k^\dagger, c_p^\dagger\} = 0, \qquad c_k^\dagger = (c_k)^\dagger.
\end{equation}
Following the notation conventions, the symbols $c_k, c_k^\dagger$ are reserved for momentum modes.

\section{Spatial Fermions via the Unitary Fourier Transform}

In continuous quantum field theory, field operators $\psi(x)$ are operator-valued distributions that must be smeared with test functions. On our discrete spatial circle $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}/L\mathbb{Z}$, spatial fermions are well-defined bounded operators constructed as finite linear combinations of momentum modes.

Recall from Chapter~\ref{ch:fourier} the real normalization factor $a \coloneqq 1/\sqrt{L} \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}$ satisfying $L a^2 = 1$, and the canonical primitive $L$-th root $\zeta = e^{2\pi i / L}$.

\begin{definition}[Spatial Fermionic Operators]
For every spatial site $x \in \Lambda$, the spatial annihilation operator $\psi_x$ and spatial creation operator $\psi_x^\dagger$ are defined by:
\begin{align}
\psi_x &\coloneqq \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} \chi(k, x)\, c_k = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} \zeta^{k x_{\mathrm{val}}}\, c_k, \label{eq:spatial_psi} \\
\psi_x^\dagger &\coloneqq \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} \chi(-k, x)\, c_k^\dagger = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} \zeta^{-k x_{\mathrm{val}}}\, c_k^\dagger. \label{eq:spatial_psidag}
\end{align}
The local spatial fermion number operator is defined by:
\begin{equation}
n_x \coloneqq \psi_x^\dagger \psi_x.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

This definition assigns the positive Fourier phase $\chi(k, x) = \zeta^{k x_{\mathrm{val}}}$ to the annihilation operator $\psi_x$. Taking the Hilbert adjoint of this linear combination naturally yields the negative phase $\chi(-k, x) = \zeta^{-k x_{\mathrm{val}}}$ for the creation operator $\psi_x^\dagger$. We note an unresolved discrepancy with the frontmatter notation conventions, which define the annihilator using the negative phase $\chi(-k, x)$. Both choices correspond to valid unitary changes of basis, but they represent opposite conventions for the discrete Fourier analysis and synthesis transforms. To match the formalized proofs in this chapter, we retain the positive-phase annihilator convention locally, without making a global sign replacement.

\begin{lemma}[Hilbert Adjoint Compatibility]
The spatial creation operator is the exact Hilbert adjoint of the spatial annihilation operator:
\begin{equation}
(\psi_x)^\dagger = \psi_x^\dagger \quad \text{for all } x \in \Lambda.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Taking the Hilbert adjoint of the linear combination~\eqref{eq:spatial_psi}:
\[
(\psi_x)^\dagger = \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} \zeta^{k x_{\mathrm{val}}} c_k \right)^\dagger = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} \overline{\zeta^{k x_{\mathrm{val}}}} (c_k)^\dagger.
\]
Because $(c_k)^\dagger = c_k^\dagger$ and $\overline{\zeta^{k x_{\mathrm{val}}}} = \zeta^{-k x_{\mathrm{val}}} = \chi(-k, x)$, this sum coincides with the definition of $\psi_x^\dagger$ in~\eqref{eq:spatial_psidag}.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact Position CAR Inheritance}

A primary consequence of this unitary equivalence is that the spatial fermions satisfy local canonical anticommutation relations.

\begin{theorem}[Exact Spatial CAR]
\label{thm:spatial_car}
For all spatial sites $x, y \in \Lambda$, the spatial operators satisfy:
\begin{align}
\{\psi_x, \psi_y^\dagger\} &= \delta_{x, y} I, \label{eq:pos_car_mixed} \\
\{\psi_x, \psi_y\} &= 0, \label{eq:pos_car_ann} \\
\{\psi_x^\dagger, \psi_y^\dagger\} &= 0. \label{eq:pos_car_cre}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We evaluate each anticommutator using the bilinearity of $\{\cdot, \cdot\}$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item For the mixed anticommutator:
    \begin{align*}
    \{\psi_x, \psi_y^\dagger\} &= \left\{ \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} \chi(k, x) c_k, \; \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{p \in \Lambda^*} \chi(-p, y) c_p^\dagger \right\} \\
    &= \frac{1}{L} \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} \sum_{p \in \Lambda^*} \chi(k, x) \chi(-p, y) \{c_k, c_p^\dagger\}.
    \end{align*}
    Substituting the momentum CAR $\{c_k, c_p^\dagger\} = \delta_{k, p} I$:
    \[
    \{\psi_x, \psi_y^\dagger\} = \frac{1}{L} \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} \chi(k, x) \chi(-k, y) I = \frac{1}{L} \left( \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} \zeta^{k (x_{\mathrm{val}} - y_{\mathrm{val}})} \right) I.
    \]
    By Theorem~\ref{thm:fourier_orthogonality}(2) (dual character orthogonality), the momentum sum equals $L\, \delta_{x, y}$. Therefore:
    \[
    \{\psi_x, \psi_y^\dagger\} = \frac{1}{L} (L\, \delta_{x, y}) I = \delta_{x, y} I.
    \]

    \item For the annihilation anticommutator:
    \[
    \{\psi_x, \psi_y\} = \frac{1}{L} \sum_{k, p \in \Lambda^*} \chi(k, x) \chi(p, y) \{c_k, c_p\} = 0,
    \]
    because $\{c_k, c_p\} = 0$ for all $k, p \in \Lambda^*$. Taking adjoints yields $\{\psi_x^\dagger, \psi_y^\dagger\} = 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Formal Correctness vs.\ Physical Implications: Global Fourier vs.\ Jordan--Wigner.}
In standard 1D lattice physics (such as Lieb, Schultz, and Mattis~\cite{lieb1961}), lattice fermions are frequently introduced through Jordan--Wigner transformations from Pauli spin matrices $\sigma_j^\pm$:
\[
\psi_j = \left( \prod_{l < j} \sigma_l^z \right) \sigma_j^-.
\]
While physically intuitive for quantum spin chains, Jordan--Wigner strings introduce non-local phase tails that can complicate spatial locality on periodic rings. In the present formulation, spatial operators $\psi_x$ are defined as global Fourier transforms of the momentum CAR generators. They anticommute locally across distinct sites, $\{\psi_x, \psi_y^\dagger\} = \delta_{x, y} I$, as a consequence of finite character orthogonality rather than through explicit string operators.

\section{Operator Fourier Inversion}

The relationship between position and momentum fermions is invertible via the inverse discrete Fourier transform.

\begin{theorem}[Fourier Inversion for Operators]
\label{thm:op_fourier_inversion}
The momentum operators are recovered from spatial fermions via the inverse discrete Fourier transform:
\begin{align}
c_k &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{x \in \Lambda} \chi(-k, x)\, \psi_x = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{x \in \Lambda} \zeta^{-k x_{\mathrm{val}}}\, \psi_x, \label{eq:inv_ck} \\
c_k^\dagger &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{x \in \Lambda} \chi(k, x)\, \psi_x^\dagger = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{x \in \Lambda} \zeta^{k x_{\mathrm{val}}}\, \psi_x^\dagger. \label{eq:inv_ckdag}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Substitute the definition of $\psi_x$ from~\eqref{eq:spatial_psi} into the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:inv_ck}:
\[
\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{x \in \Lambda} \chi(-k, x) \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{p \in \Lambda^*} \chi(p, x) c_p \right) = \frac{1}{L} \sum_{p \in \Lambda^*} \left( \sum_{x \in \Lambda} \chi(p - k, x) \right) c_p.
\]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:fourier_orthogonality}(1) (spatial character orthogonality), $\sum_{x \in \Lambda} \chi(p - k, x) = L\, \delta_{p, k}$. Thus:
\[
\frac{1}{L} \sum_{p \in \Lambda^*} (L\, \delta_{p, k}) c_p = c_k.
\]
Taking Hilbert adjoints yields the corresponding identity for $c_k^\dagger$.
\end{proof}

\section{Conservation of Total Particle Number}

\begin{theorem}[Particle Number Invariance]
\label{thm:number_invariance}
The total particle number operator $\hat{N}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\hat{N}_{\mathrm{tot}} \coloneqq \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} c_k^\dagger c_k = \sum_{x \in \Lambda} \psi_x^\dagger \psi_x = \sum_{x \in \Lambda} n_x.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Expand the sum of spatial number operators using~\eqref{eq:spatial_psi} and~\eqref{eq:spatial_psidag}:
\begin{align*}
\sum_{x \in \Lambda} \psi_x^\dagger \psi_x &= \sum_{x \in \Lambda} \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} \chi(-k, x) c_k^\dagger \right) \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \sum_{p \in \Lambda^*} \chi(p, x) c_p \right) \\
&= \frac{1}{L} \sum_{k, p \in \Lambda^*} c_k^\dagger c_p \left( \sum_{x \in \Lambda} \chi(p - k, x) \right).
\end{align*}
Applying spatial character orthogonality $\sum_{x \in \Lambda} \chi(p - k, x) = L\, \delta_{p, k}$:
\[
\sum_{x \in \Lambda} \psi_x^\dagger \psi_x = \frac{1}{L} \sum_{k, p \in \Lambda^*} c_k^\dagger c_p (L\, \delta_{p, k}) = \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} c_k^\dagger c_k = \hat{N}_{\mathrm{tot}}.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{The Dirac Fermi Sea and Linearized Hamiltonians}

To model physical fermions, the relevant ground state is often not the empty state $|\emptyset\rangle$, but a filled Dirac sea where all negative-energy single-particle levels are occupied.

\begin{definition}[Dirac Sea Configuration and Vacuum]
For even lattice length $L = 2h$, the half-filled Dirac Fermi sea configuration $\mathcal{S}_0 \subset \Lambda^*$ consists of all non-positive momentum modes:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}_0 \coloneqq \{k \in \Lambda^* \mid k \le 0\} = \{-h+1, -h+2, \dots, -1, 0\}.
\end{equation}
The cardinality of the Fermi sea is $\#\mathcal{S}_0 = h = L/2$. The Dirac sea vacuum state is the corresponding occupation state:
\begin{equation}
|\Omega\rangle \coloneqq |\mathcal{S}_0\rangle \in \mathcal{F}(L).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Bare and Physical Momentum Hamiltonians]
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The bare kinetic Hamiltonian $H_0$ is defined by:
    \begin{equation}
    H_0 \coloneqq \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} k\, c_k^\dagger c_k = \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} k\, n_k.
    \end{equation}
    \item The ground state sea energy $E_\Omega$ is the eigenvalue of $H_0$ on $|\Omega\rangle$:
    \begin{equation}
    E_\Omega \coloneqq \sum_{k \in \mathcal{S}_0} k.
    \end{equation}
    \item The physical, normal-ordered Hamiltonian $\hat{P}$ is defined by:
    \begin{equation}
    \hat{P} \coloneqq H_0 - E_\Omega \cdot I,
    \end{equation}
    so that $\hat{P} |\Omega\rangle = 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Evaluation of Ground State Energy]
For $L = 2h$ with $h \ge 1$, the bare ground state energy evaluates to:
\begin{equation}
E_\Omega = -\frac{h(h-1)}{2}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By definition, $\mathcal{S}_0 = \{-h+1, \dots, 0\}$. Summing the consecutive negative integers:
\[
E_\Omega = \sum_{j=-h+1}^0 j = - \sum_{m=1}^{h-1} m = - \frac{(h-1) h}{2} = -\frac{h(h-1)}{2}.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Spectra and Commutation Relations]
\label{thm:h0_commutators}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item On any occupation basis state $|S\rangle$ ($S \subseteq \Lambda^*$):
    \begin{equation}
    H_0 |S\rangle = \left( \sum_{k \in S} k \right) |S\rangle, \qquad \hat{P} |S\rangle = \left( \sum_{k \in S} k - E_\Omega \right) |S\rangle.
    \end{equation}
    \item For any mode $p \in \Lambda^*$, the creation and annihilation operators satisfy:
    \begin{align}
    [H_0, c_p^\dagger] &= [\hat{P}, c_p^\dagger] = p\, c_p^\dagger, \\
    [H_0, c_p] &= [\hat{P}, c_p] = -p\, c_p.
    \end{align}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We prove each statement:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Each $n_k$ is diagonal on basis states: $n_k |S\rangle = |S\rangle$ if $k \in S$, and $0$ otherwise. Summing over $k \in \Lambda^*$ gives $H_0 |S\rangle = (\sum_{k \in S} k) |S\rangle$. Subtracting $E_\Omega I$ gives the eigenvalue for $\hat{P}$.
    \item Using the bilinear CAR commutator $[n_k, c_p^\dagger] = \delta_{k, p} c_p^\dagger$ from Theorem~\ref{thm:algebraic_car}(2):
    \[
    [H_0, c_p^\dagger] = \left[ \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} k\, n_k, \; c_p^\dagger \right] = \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} k\, [n_k, c_p^\dagger] = \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} k (\delta_{k, p} c_p^\dagger) = p\, c_p^\dagger.
    \]
    Similarly, $[n_k, c_p] = -\delta_{k, p} c_p$ yields $[H_0, c_p] = -p\, c_p$.
    Because $\hat{P} = H_0 - E_\Omega I$ differs from $H_0$ only by a central scalar multiple of the identity, the commutators with $\hat{P}$ are identical.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Formal Correctness vs.\ Physical Implications: Linearization and the Dirac Sea.}
In standard Luttinger liquid theory (e.g., Haldane~\cite{haldane1981}, Giamarchi~\cite{giamarchi2004}, and von Delft--Schoeller~\cite{vondelft1998}), the kinetic dispersion is linearized near the Fermi points $\pm k_F$. The spectrum is extended to $-\infty$, yielding an infinite Dirac sea. While this approximation simplifies the evaluation of commutators, it results in a formally infinite ground state energy $\sum_{k \le 0} k = -\infty$, which is handled via infinite normal ordering.

In the present finite-dimensional setting, the Dirac sea $\mathcal{S}_0$ has a lower bound at mode $-h+1$. The ground state energy $E_\Omega = -h(h-1)/2$ is a finite integer, so no infinite subtraction is necessary. For excitations near the Fermi surface $k \approx 0$, the dispersion remains strictly linear ($\epsilon_k = k$), modeling the low-energy behavior of chiral 1D fermions within the explicitly bounded momentum band.

\section{Chapter Summary and Literature Comparison}

In this chapter, we united the Fourier transform and CAR representations to describe lattice fermions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Spatial fermionic operators $\psi_x, \psi_x^\dagger$ are constructed via the discrete Fourier transform of momentum modes $c_k, c_k^\dagger$.
    \item Spatial operators satisfy local CAR relations $\{\psi_x, \psi_y^\dagger\} = \delta_{x, y} I$ as a consequence of finite character orthogonality.
    \item Total particle number is conserved across representations: $\hat{N}_{\mathrm{tot}} = \sum_k c_k^\dagger c_k = \sum_x \psi_x^\dagger \psi_x$.
    \item The half-filled Dirac sea $\mathcal{S}_0$ provides a finite ground state $|\Omega\rangle$ with finite energy $E_\Omega = -h(h-1)/2$.
    \item The physical momentum Hamiltonian $\hat{P} = H_0 - E_\Omega I$ generates energy shifts $[\hat{P}, c_p^\dagger] = p c_p^\dagger$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Comparison with Standard Continuum Literature.}
In texts such as Giamarchi~\cite{giamarchi2004} and Gogolin et al.~\cite{gogolin1998}, spatial fermions $\psi(x)$ and momentum modes $c_k$ are often introduced with continuous spatial indices $x \in [0, \mathcal{L}]$. Field operators then satisfy distributional anticommutators $\{\psi(x), \psi^\dagger(y)\} = \delta(x-y)$. In the finite formulation used here, the lattice fermions $\psi_x$ are bounded operators on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space. All sums are finite, operator products are mathematically well-defined, and the Dirac sea is a bounded set.

These spatial fermions provide the foundation for building observable algebras localized to spatial intervals. In Chapter~\ref{ch:lattice_aqft_net}, we use these operators to construct a graded local net, distinguishing between the field locality of the anticommuting fermions and the ordinary spatial locality of even observables.
