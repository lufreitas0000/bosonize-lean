\section{Introduction and the Algebraic Origin of the Schwinger Anomaly}

A key step in the standard bosonization program is establishing that bilinear products of fermionic field operators satisfy bosonic canonical commutation relations (CCR). In continuous quantum field theory, this phenomenon is expressed by the affine Lie algebra $\widehat{\mathfrak{u}}(1)$ (the $U(1)$ Kac--Moody current algebra):
\begin{equation}
[J_m, J_n] = m\, \delta_{m+n, 0}\, I.
\end{equation}
The central term $m\, \delta_{m+n, 0}$, known as the \emph{Schwinger term} or the chiral anomaly, is traditionally derived in infinite-dimensional settings via point-splitting, formal normal ordering, or infinite Dirac sea subtractions.

Our model operates on a discrete lattice $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}/L\mathbb{Z}$ ($L = 2h$, $h \ge 1$) with strictly finite-dimensional carrier spaces. As stated by the No-Go Theorem~\ref{thm:no_go}, a global nonzero scalar canonical commutation relation cannot hold for finite matrices, because the trace of any commutator must vanish identically on a finite-dimensional space. We must therefore carefully distinguish between a dynamical identity on the full finite Fock space $\mathcal{F}(L)$, a protected-budget identity valid only on restricted subspaces, and the ambient polynomial algebra.

To recover the exact Schwinger term, we rely on the \emph{Energy Budget regularization} applied to finite subspaces:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item On the full finite-dimensional Fock space $\mathcal{F}(L)$, the commutator $[\rho_{-m}, \rho_m]$ is not a scalar. Instead, it is a dynamical operator determined by the difference between the fermion occupations at the global bottom and top edges of the momentum band. We evaluate these global edge terms explicitly before considering any subspace restrictions.
    \item We then restrict our attention to the energy budget subspace $B(N, K)$. Provided the parameters satisfy explicit \emph{margin inequalities}, the deep Fermi sea remains rigidly full and the high momentum band remains rigidly empty.
    \item As a result, the dynamical edge operators evaluate to exactly $m$ when acting on these protected budget states. This establishes a protected-budget CCR identity that locally matches the Kac--Moody algebra, even though the global operator on $\mathcal{F}(L)$ remains traceless.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The Exact Diagonal Edge Formula on Fock Space}

We first evaluate the commutator of opposing density modes on the entire Fock space $\mathcal{F}(L)$ without any subspace restriction.

Recall that $\rho_m = d\Gamma(T_m)$ is the second quantization of the one-particle partial shift matrix $T_m$ on $\ell^2(\Lambda^*)$ (Chapter~\ref{ch:density_modes}). By Lemma~\ref{lem:dgamma_comm}, second quantization preserves Lie brackets:
\begin{equation}
[\rho_{-m}, \rho_m] = d\Gamma([T_{-m}, T_m]).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact Diagonal Edge Formula]
\label{thm:diagonal_edge_formula}
Let $L = 2h$ with $h \ge 1$. For any integer $1 \le m \le h$, the algebraic commutator $[\rho_{-m}, \rho_m]$ on the entire Fock space $\mathcal{F}(L)$ satisfies the exact operator identity:
\begin{equation}
[\rho_{-m}, \rho_m] = \sum_{q = -h+1}^{-h+m} n_q - \sum_{q = h-m+1}^{h} n_q,
\end{equation}
where $n_q \coloneqq c_q^\dagger c_q$ is the momentum occupation number operator at mode $q$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We evaluate the commutator of the one-particle shift matrices $[T_{-m}, T_m] = T_{-m} T_m - T_m T_{-m}$ in $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\ell^2(\Lambda^*))$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The matrix elements of $T_m$ and $T_{-m}$ are:
    \[
    (T_m)_{p, k} = \begin{cases} 1, & \text{if } p = k + m, \\ 0, & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases} \qquad
    (T_{-m})_{p, k} = \begin{cases} 1, & \text{if } p = k - m, \\ 0, & \text{otherwise}. \end{cases}
    \]
    \item Compute the diagonal elements of the product $T_{-m} T_m$:
    \[
    (T_{-m} T_m)_{q, q} = \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} (T_{-m})_{q, k} (T_m)_{k, q}.
    \]
    The term is non-zero if and only if $q = k - m$ and $k = q + m$. This requires that both $q \in \Lambda^*$ and $q + m \in \Lambda^*$.
    Since $\Lambda^* = \{-h+1, \dots, h\}$, $q + m \in \Lambda^*$ requires $q + m \le h \iff q \le h - m$.
    Therefore:
    \[
    (T_{-m} T_m)_{q, q} = \begin{cases} 1, & \text{if } -h+1 \le q \le h - m, \\ 0, & \text{if } h - m < q \le h. \end{cases}
    \]
    \item Similarly, compute the diagonal elements of $T_m T_{-m}$:
    \[
    (T_m T_{-m})_{q, q} = \sum_{k \in \Lambda^*} (T_m)_{q, k} (T_{-m})_{k, q}.
    \]
    This is non-zero if and only if $q = k + m$ and $k = q - m$, which requires $q - m \in \Lambda^* \iff q - m \ge -h+1 \iff q \ge -h+1+m$.
    Therefore:
    \[
    (T_m T_{-m})_{q, q} = \begin{cases} 1, & \text{if } -h+1+m \le q \le h, \\ 0, & \text{if } -h+1 \le q < -h+1+m. \end{cases}
    \]
    \item Off-diagonal elements $(T_{-m} T_m)_{p, q}$ with $p \neq q$ require $p = k - m$ and $k = q + m \implies p = q$, so all off-diagonal elements vanish identically.
    \item Subtracting the two diagonal matrices:
    \[
    [T_{-m}, T_m]_{q, q} = (T_{-m} T_m)_{q, q} - (T_m T_{-m})_{q, q}.
    \]
    For bulk modes in the interior $-h+1+m \le q \le h-m$, both terms equal $1$, so their difference vanishes: $1 - 1 = 0$.
    The only non-zero entries occur at the boundaries:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item For bottom edge modes $-h+1 \le q \le -h+m$: $(T_{-m} T_m)_{q, q} = 1$ and $(T_m T_{-m})_{q, q} = 0$, giving $+1$.
        \item For top edge modes $h-m < q \le h$: $(T_{-m} T_m)_{q, q} = 0$ and $(T_m T_{-m})_{q, q} = 1$, giving $-1$.
    \end{itemize}
    Therefore, the matrix commutator is diagonal:
    \[
    [T_{-m}, T_m] = \sum_{q=-h+1}^{-h+m} E_{q, q} - \sum_{q=h-m+1}^h E_{q, q},
    \]
    where $E_{q, q}$ is the elementary diagonal projection matrix.
    \item Applying second quantization $d\Gamma(E_{q, q}) = c_q^\dagger c_q = n_q$ yields the theorem.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Edge Domains]
For any mode $1 \le m \le h$, the \emph{Bottom Edge Domain} $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{bot}}(m)$ and \emph{Top Edge Domain} $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{top}}(m)$ are the subsets of $\Lambda^*$:
\begin{align}
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{bot}}(m) &\coloneqq \{ q \in \Lambda^* \mid q \le -h + m \} = \{-h+1, -h+2, \dots, -h+m\}, \\
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{top}}(m) &\coloneqq \{ q \in \Lambda^* \mid q > h - m \} = \{h - m + 1, \dots, h\}.
\end{align}
Both sets contain exactly $m$ momentum modes: $\#\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{bot}}(m) = \#\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{top}}(m) = m$.
\end{definition}

\section{Regime M1: The Diagonal Buffer Zone and the Schwinger Term}

Having established the global edge formula for $[\rho_{-m}, \rho_m]$ on the unrestricted Fock space, we now evaluate its action on states belonging to the constrained energy budget subspace $B(N, K)$. By introducing a margin condition (M1), we ensure that the global edge terms act on regions of the momentum band where particle occupations are rigidly blocked by the finite budget.

\begin{theorem}[Regime M1: Exact Diagonal Schwinger Term]
\label{thm:regime_m1}
Let $\psi \in B(N, K)$ be any state in the energy budget with charge $N \in \{-h, \dots, h\}$ and excitation energy at most $K \ge 0$.
If the mode $m \ge 1$ satisfies the \textbf{M1 Margin Condition}:
\begin{equation}
m + K + |N| \le h,
\end{equation}
then the commutator $[\rho_{-m}, \rho_m]$ evaluates on $\psi$ to the exact scalar Schwinger term:
\begin{equation}
[\rho_{-m}, \rho_m] \psi = m \psi.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every vector $\psi \in B(N, K)$ is a linear combination of basis vectors $|S\rangle$ satisfying $N(S) = N$ and $e(S) \le K$. By linearity, it suffices to prove the identity on an arbitrary such basis state $|S\rangle$.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:diagonal_edge_formula}:
\[
[\rho_{-m}, \rho_m] |S\rangle = \left( \sum_{q \in \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{bot}}(m)} n_q - \sum_{q \in \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{top}}(m)} n_q \right) |S\rangle.
\]
We evaluate the action of the number operators on $|S\rangle$ using the Frozen Margins Theorem~\ref{thm:frozen_margins}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item For any mode $q \in \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{bot}}(m)$, $q \le -h + m$.
    Using the M1 condition $m \le h - K - |N|$, we have:
    \[
    q \le -h + m \le -h + (h - K - |N|) = - K - |N| \le N - K,
    \]
    since $-|N| \le N$.
    By Theorem~\ref{thm:frozen_margins}(1) (Deep Sea Rigidity), every mode $q \le N - K$ is rigidly occupied in $S$, so $q \in S$.
    Therefore, $n_q |S\rangle = |S\rangle$ for every $q \in \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{bot}}(m)$.
    Summing over the $m$ modes of $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{bot}}(m)$:
    \[
    \sum_{q \in \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{bot}}(m)} n_q |S\rangle = \sum_{q \in \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{bot}}(m)} |S\rangle = m |S\rangle.
    \]

    \item For any mode $q \in \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{top}}(m)$, $q \ge h - m + 1 > h - m$.
    Using the M1 condition $m \le h - K - |N|$, we have:
    \[
    q > h - m \ge h - (h - K - |N|) = K + |N| \ge N + K,
    \]
    since $|N| \ge N$.
    By Theorem~\ref{thm:frozen_margins}(2) (High Momentum Rigidity), every mode $q > N + K$ is rigidly empty in $S$, so $q \notin S$.
    Therefore, $n_q |S\rangle = 0$ for every $q \in \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{top}}(m)$.
    Summing over the $m$ modes of $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{top}}(m)$:
    \[
    \sum_{q \in \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{top}}(m)} n_q |S\rangle = 0.
    \]
\end{enumerate}
Subtracting the two sums yields:
\[
[\rho_{-m}, \rho_m] |S\rangle = (m - 0) |S\rangle = m |S\rangle.
\]
Extending by linearity to all $\psi \in B(N, K)$ completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\section{Regime M2: Off-Diagonal Suppression and Pauli Blocking}

The diagonal case analyzed in Regime M1 successfully resolves the Schwinger term for opposing density modes. We now turn to the off-diagonal commutators $[\rho_m, \rho_n]$ where $m + n \neq 0$. Here, we demonstrate how an extended margin condition (M2) ensures that any single-particle hopping terms connecting the opposing band edges are annihilated by Pauli blocking on the rigid ground sea configurations.

\begin{theorem}[Regime M2: Off-Diagonal Suppression]
\label{thm:regime_m2}
Let $\psi \in B(N, K)$. For any mode transfers $m, n \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $m + n \neq 0$, if the parameters satisfy the \textbf{M2 Margin Condition}:
\begin{equation}
|m| + |n| + K + |N| \le h,
\end{equation}
then the density modes commute identically on $\psi$:
\begin{equation}
[\rho_m, \rho_n] \psi = 0.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We divide into two cases:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Same-Sign Modes:} If $m$ and $n$ have the same sign (both non-negative or both non-positive), then by Theorem~\ref{thm:same_sign_comm}, $[\rho_m, \rho_n] = 0$ globally on the entire Fock space, so $[\rho_m, \rho_n] \psi = 0$ holds without any margin condition.
    \item \textbf{Opposite-Sign Modes:} Assume without loss of generality that $m > 0$ and $n = -k < 0$ with $m \neq k$.
    Evaluating the single-particle commutator $[T_m, T_{-k}]$ yields non-zero matrix elements only at single-particle hops $c_p^\dagger c_q$ where $|p - q| = |m - k| > 0$ with either $q$ lying in the top edge or $p$ lying in the bottom edge.
    Under the M2 margin condition $|m| + |k| + K + |N| \le h$, any hopping operator originating from the top edge has an empty source ($q > N + K$, so $c_q |S\rangle = 0$), and any hopping operator landing in the bottom edge has a full destination ($p \le N - K$, so $c_p^\dagger |S\rangle = 0$ due to Pauli blocking $p \in S$).
    Therefore, every off-diagonal hopping operator annihilates $|S\rangle$.
    Summing all terms yields $[\rho_m, \rho_n] \psi = 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\section[The Exact U(1) Kac--Moody Algebra on the Budget Subspace]{The Exact $U(1)$ Kac--Moody Algebra on the Budget Subspace}

Combining the diagonal Schwinger term from Regime M1 and the off-diagonal suppression from Regime M2 yields a protected commutation relation on the finite budget. Note the choice of sign and index order in the resulting relation: the theorem establishes $[\rho_m, \rho_n] = -m \delta_{m+n, 0}$ for normal-ordered modes. This negative sign stems from the explicit definitions of our finite shift operations on the spatial lattice and the orientation of the modes; it must be mapped carefully when comparing to literature conventions that assume positive scalars.

\begin{theorem}[$U(1)$ Kac--Moody Current Algebra on $B(N, K)$]
\label{thm:kac_moody}
Let $M \ge 1$ be a mode cutoff. Under the uniform margin condition:
\begin{equation}
2M + K + |N| \le h,
\end{equation}
the normal-ordered density modes $:\!\rho_m\!:$ satisfy the exact Kac--Moody algebra for all $|m|, |n| \le M$ on the budget subspace $B(N, K)$:
\begin{equation}
\left[ :\!\rho_m\!:, :\!\rho_n\!: \right] \psi = -m\, \delta_{m+n, 0}\, \psi \quad \text{for all } \psi \in B(N, K).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, setting $n = -m$ with $m \ge 1$:
\begin{equation}
\left[ :\!\rho_{-m}\!:, :\!\rho_m\!: \right] \psi = m \psi.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $|m|, |n| \le M$.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item If $m = 0$ or $n = 0$: $:\!\rho_0\!: = \hat{N}$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:charge_energy_covariance}(1), $[\hat{N}, \rho_m] = 0$, so $[:\!\rho_0\!:, :\!\rho_n\!:] = 0 = -0 \cdot \delta_{0+n, 0} I$.
    \item If $m + n \neq 0$: since $|m| + |n| \le 2M$, the uniform condition $2M + K + |N| \le h$ implies the M2 margin condition $|m| + |n| + K + |N| \le h$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:regime_m2}, $[:\!\rho_m\!:, :\!\rho_n\!:] \psi = 0 = -m \delta_{m+n, 0} \psi$.
    \item If $m + n = 0$ with $m \ge 1$ (so $n = -m$): the condition $2M + K + |N| \le h$ implies $m + K + |N| \le h$ (since $m \le M \le 2M$).
    By Theorem~\ref{thm:regime_m1}, $[\rho_{-m}, \rho_m] \psi = m \psi$.
    By antisymmetry of the commutator:
    \[
    [:\!\rho_m\!:, :\!\rho_{-m}\!:] \psi = - [:\!\rho_{-m}\!:, :\!\rho_m\!:] \psi = - m \psi = -m\, \delta_{m+(-m), 0} \psi.
    \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Harmonic Oscillator Bosonic Modes]
For each mode $m \ge 1$, we define the normalized bosonic creation and annihilation operators on $B(N, K)$ by:
\begin{equation}
b_m \coloneqq \frac{1}{\sqrt{m}} :\!\rho_{-m}\!:, \qquad b_m^\dagger \coloneqq \frac{1}{\sqrt{m}} :\!\rho_m\!: = (b_m)^\dagger.
\end{equation}
Under the margin condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:kac_moody}, these operators satisfy the standard harmonic oscillator algebra on $B(N, K)$:
\begin{equation}
[b_m, b_n^\dagger] \psi = \delta_{m, n} \psi, \qquad [b_m, b_n] \psi = 0, \qquad [b_m^\dagger, b_n^\dagger] \psi = 0.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\section{Word-Excursion Tracking in Composed Products}

When sequentially applying multiple density operators $\rho_{a_1} \rho_{a_2} \cdots \rho_{a_s}$ to a state (a procedure required to build vertex operators or multi-mode partition states), the intermediate states undergo transient energy fluctuations. For the protected budget commutator to hold universally across the entire evaluation of such an operator word, the intermediate states are not required to remain in the original initial budget $B(N, K)$. Instead, they enter an enlarged budget controlled by the \emph{maximum upward energy excursion} encountered during the sequential application. The overall margin inequality involving the finite band edge $h$ must hold for this enlarged maximum budget.

\begin{definition}[Application Order and Cumulative Energy Excursion]
For a written operator product $W = \rho_{a_1} \rho_{a_2} \cdots \rho_{a_s}$, the operators act from right to left in the sequence of signed transfers $(a_s, a_{s-1}, \dots, a_1)$.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The partial energy shift after applying the first $r$ operators ($0 \le r \le s$) is:
    \begin{equation}
    \Delta E_r \coloneqq \sum_{j=1}^r a_{s - j + 1}.
    \end{equation}
    \item The \emph{maximum upward energy excursion} $X(W)$ is:
    \begin{equation}
    X(W) \coloneqq \max\left(0, \; \max_{0 \le r \le s} \Delta E_r \right).
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Valid Margin Excursion Accounting]
\label{thm:excursion_accounting}
Let $W = \rho_{a_1} \dots \rho_{a_s}$ be an operator word with mode cutoff $|a_j| \le M$. If the input state $\psi \in B(N, K)$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
2M + K + X(W) + |N| \le h,
\end{equation}
then every intermediate state generated during the evaluation of $W \psi$ belongs to $B(N, K + X(W))$, and every commutator $[:\!\rho_m\!:, :\!\rho_n\!:]$ encountered along the evaluation acts as the exact scalar $-m \delta_{m+n, 0}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:budget_action_annihilation}, applying $\rho_{a_s}$ to $\psi \in B(N, K)$ produces a state in $B(N, K + a_s)$. By induction, after $r$ steps the state belongs to $B(N, K + \Delta E_r) \subseteq B(N, K + X(W))$.
Since $2M + (K + X(W)) + |N| \le h$, each intermediate state satisfies the uniform margin condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:kac_moody}. Therefore, all intermediate commutators evaluate to exact scalars.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Formalization Note]
In the verified Lean~4 development (as formalized in Chapter~10 and Appendix~A04), all edge formulas, margin bounds, and excursion trackers are certified constructively without noncomputable axioms. The reconciliation of the finite-dimensional trace obstruction is precise: on the bare empty state $|\emptyset\rangle$, the commutator $[\rho_{-m}, \rho_m] |\emptyset\rangle = 0 \neq m |\emptyset\rangle$. The commutator is strictly traceless across the full Fock carrier $\mathcal{F}(L)$, yet evaluates identically to the non-zero central scalar $m I$ on the physical low-energy budget subspace $B(N, K)$.
\end{remark}

\section{From Protected Algebra to State Space Spanning}

The margin-protected identity in Theorem~\ref{thm:kac_moody} is an input-restricted ambient-operator identity, not a closed finite-dimensional endomorphism representation of the CCR. Because raising currents generally map states out of a fixed budget, composition of multiple modes is performed in the ambient finite CAR space with explicitly tracked margins. Establishing that these density modes locally obey a bosonic algebra subject to these margins does not immediately imply that the multi-mode excitations they generate are sufficient to describe every valid state. 

A critical question remains: when we apply the positive density modes $\rho_m$ to the sector ground state $|N\rangle_0$, do the resulting bosonic partition states span the entirety of the protected budget subspace $B(N, K)$, or are there hidden fermionic degrees of freedom left out? To answer this, we must compare the dimension of the subspace created by these bosonic operators with the exact combinatorics of the underlying fermionic particle-hole excitations. This counting problem, which determines the completeness of the bosonization map on the finite lattice, is the subject of Chapter~\ref{ch:haldane_completeness}.
