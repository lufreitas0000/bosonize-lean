\chapter*{Preface}
\markboth{Preface}{Preface}

This monograph develops a constructive account of $1+1$-dimensional bosonization on a finite lattice. Its starting point is physical: continuum descriptions bring divergent expressions and infinite mode sums that require regularization and careful limiting arguments. A finite periodic chain replaces those expressions with finite sums and finite-dimensional state spaces, so each construction can be stated and analyzed before any continuum limit is considered.

The finite setting makes the algebraic constraints of bosonization explicit. Periodic boundary conditions identify momenta modulo the lattice length; the finite momentum band has a distinguished boundary mode; and fermionic operators act on specified finite-dimensional sectors. Density commutators and bosonic representations therefore come with precise domains, cutoffs, and energy budgets. These restrictions are part of the physical statements: they identify where a relation holds exactly and where finite-band effects enter. The energy budget also provides a regularization by restricting attention to states of bounded excitation energy, while keeping the underlying lattice and its operator algebra exact.

The finite theory does not, by itself, identify every result with its continuum counterpart. Each chapter states its finite carriers, charge sectors, cutoffs, and margin hypotheses, and distinguishes exact lattice identities from interpretations or limiting claims. The goal is to make the finite mathematics and its physical content transparent enough that readers can see both what is controlled and what requires an additional argument.
