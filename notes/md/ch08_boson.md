# BOSONIZE-LEAN: Mathematical Reference Notes

## Part I: Phase 1 Foundations

### Chapter 8: The Umbral Boson Fock Layer

*Context: This chapter represents the capstone of Phase 1. It constructs a purely algebraic framework for bosonic operators. It is a general mathematical construction, independent of any fermionic system, which will serve as the target space for the exact bosonization mapping in Phase 2.*

To sidestep the formidable analytic subtleties of infinite-dimensional topological vector spaces—such as the domain issues of unbounded operators and infinite-dimensional trace paradoxes—we employ an **umbral (polynomial) approach** for the bosonic sector. The bosonic Fock space is realized purely algebraically, and creation/annihilation operators are globally defined endomorphisms on a multivariate polynomial ring.

#### 8.1 The Algebraic Setting: Polynomials vs. Formal Power Series

In this formalization, we must choose between the ring of multivariate polynomials and the ring of formal power series.
**We strictly choose polynomials.**
A formal power series allows infinite sums (e.g., $\sum_{n=0}^\infty X^n$), which in Lean requires topological completion or limits to evaluate globally. Polynomials (in Lean, `MvPolynomial`) strictly guarantee that every individual element has a *finite* maximum degree.

However, to perform exact computations on operators (like exponentials) and to model the finite lattice physics properly, relying on the arbitrary finite degree of individual polynomials is not enough. We must impose a **strict, global hard cap $K$** (the energy budget). By bounding the maximum degree across the entire working space, we slice the infinite-dimensional polynomial ring into strict, finite-dimensional vector spaces. On these bounded slices, derivative operators become strictly nilpotent, and infinite series like the exponential truncate exactly without requiring convergence limits.

#### 8.2 The $M=1$ Example: The Single Harmonic Oscillator and the Global Cap

To build intuition, consider a single mode $M=1$. The algebraic carrier space (the Fock space $\mathcal{F}$) is simply $\mathbb{C}[X]$, the ring of polynomials in one variable.

*   **Creation ($a^\dagger$):** Multiplication by $X$. For example, $a^\dagger(X^2) = X^3$.
*   **Annihilation ($a$):** The formal derivative $\partial / \partial X$. For example, $a(X^2) = 2X$.

In standard quantum mechanics, the carrier space is $L^2(\mathbb{R})$, and the operators $\hat{x}$ and $\hat{p}$ are *unbounded*. In our umbral framework, the endomorphism space $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}[X])$ requires no topology, and the operators are globally defined. On the infinite-dimensional $\mathbb{C}[X]$, the Canonical Commutation Relation (CCR) holds exactly: $[a, a^\dagger] = I$.

**The Effect of the Global Cap $K$ and Finite Lie Algebras:**
When we apply the global cap $K$, we restrict our attention to the finite-dimensional subspace $\mathcal{F}_{\le K} = \mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{1, X, X^2, \dots, X^K\}$.
Because the space is finite-dimensional, the annihilation operator $a = \partial_X$ becomes strictly **nilpotent**: $a^{K+1} = 0$ identically.

However, the raw creation operator $a^\dagger$ maps $X^K \to X^{K+1}$, which lies *outside* $\mathcal{F}_{\le K}$. To make it a valid endomorphism on the capped space, we must apply a projection $P_K$, defining $\tilde{a}^\dagger := P_K \circ a^\dagger$. Because of this projection, $\tilde{a}^\dagger$ is *also* nilpotent: $(\tilde{a}^\dagger)^{K+1} = 0$.

This profoundly alters the Lie algebra. The finite-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra $\mathfrak{h}_1$ generated by $\{X, Y, Z\}$ with $[X, Y] = Z$ (where $Z$ is central) has **no exact non-trivial finite-dimensional representations** over fields of characteristic zero (as proven by the trace no-go theorem in Chapter 2, Lemma 2.9).
Indeed, on our capped space, the exact CCR breaks at the boundary:
$$ [a, \tilde{a}^\dagger] X^n = X^n \quad \text{for } n < K $$
$$ [a, \tilde{a}^\dagger] X^K = -K X^K $$
The trace of the commutator is $\sum_{n=0}^{K-1} 1 - K = 0$, satisfying the algebraic trace requirement while perfectly reproducing the continuum CCR for all low-energy states far from the budget cap!

#### 8.3 Definitions: Carrier Space and Operators

**Definition 8.1 (Bosonic Carrier Space).**
Let $Q = \{1, \dots, M\}$ be a finite set of positive integer mode indices. The algebraic carrier space $\mathcal{F}_b$ is the commutative ring of multivariate polynomials over $\mathbb{C}$ generated by the variables $\{X_m\}_{m \in Q}$:

$$
\mathcal{F}_b := \mathbb{C}[X_1, \dots, X_M] \tag{8.1}
$$

The vacuum state is defined as the constant polynomial $1 \in \mathcal{F}_b$.

**Definition 8.2 (Operators and Currents).**
For every mode $m \in Q$, we define the creation operator $a_m^\dagger$ and annihilation operator $a_m$ as linear endomorphisms in the module category $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{F}_b)$. For all $p \in \mathcal{F}_b$:

$$
a_m^\dagger(p) := X_m \cdot p \tag{8.2}
$$

$$
a_m(p) := \frac{\partial}{\partial X_m} p \tag{8.3}
$$

The unnormalized currents (which scale with the mode index) are:

$$
J_m^\dagger := a_m^\dagger = X_m \cdot \tag{8.4}
$$

$$
J_m := m \, a_m = m \frac{\partial}{\partial X_m} \tag{8.5}
$$

**Definition 8.3 (Bosonic Observables).**
The bosonic number operator $\hat{N}_b$ (the Euler operator) and the bosonic kinetic energy operator $H_b$ (the weighted degree operator) are defined as:

$$
\hat{N}_b := \sum_{m \in Q} a_m^\dagger a_m \tag{8.6}
$$

$$
H_b := \sum_{m \in Q} m \, a_m^\dagger a_m \tag{8.7}
$$

Equivalently, substituting the currents into the energy operator:

$$
H_b = \sum_{m \in Q} J_m^\dagger a_m \tag{8.8}
$$

#### 8.4 Filtrations and Nets

**Intuition for Filtrations:**
To perform physics on polynomials, we bound the operators using a **filtration**. A filtration is an infinite sequence of nested vector subspaces (a "net") $V_0 \subseteq V_1 \subseteq V_2 \dots$ that exhausts the whole space $\bigcup_K V_K = \mathcal{F}_b$. By restricting our attention to a specific subspace $\mathcal{F}_{\le K}$, we impose the hard cap $K$. On this finite subspace, certain operators become strictly nilpotent, and infinite series terminate exactly.

**Definition 8.4 (Weight Filtration).**
Let $\mathcal{F}_{\le K}$ be the finite-dimensional vector subspace of $\mathcal{F}_b$ consisting of polynomials whose weighted degree (energy) is strictly bounded by $K \in \mathbb{N}$:

$$
\mathcal{F}_{\le K} := \mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}} \left\{ \prod_{m \in Q} X_m^{r_m} \mathrel{\Bigg\vert{}} \sum_{m \in Q} m \cdot r_m \le K \right\} \tag{8.9}
$$

#### 8.5 Lie Algebras and Exact Commutators

**Lemma 8.5 (Exact CCR / Heisenberg Lie Algebra).**
For all $m, m' \in Q$, the Canonical Commutation Relations hold as exact algebraic identities on the infinite-dimensional polynomial ring $\mathcal{F}_b$ without domain restrictions:

$$
[a_m, a_{m'}^\dagger] = \delta_{mm'} I \tag{8.10}
$$

$$
[a_m, a_{m'}] = 0 \tag{8.11}
$$

$$
[a_m^\dagger, a_{m'}^\dagger] = 0 \tag{8.12}
$$

Consequently, $[J_m, J_{m'}^\dagger] = m \, \delta_{mm'} I$.

**Lemma 8.6 (Module Isomorphism of the Euler Derivation).**
The operator $H_b$ acts as a derivation on the algebra $\mathcal{F}_b$. Furthermore, the monomials form a complete basis of eigenvectors for $H_b$. For any monomial $p = \prod X_m^{r_m}$:

$$
H_b (p) = \left( \sum_{m \in Q} m \cdot r_m \right) p \tag{8.13}
$$

#### 8.6 Operator Identities, Normal Ordering, and Wick's Theorem

**Lemma 8.7 (Commutation of Powers).**
For any $m, m' \in Q$ and any strictly positive integer $n \in \mathbb{N}$, the commutator follows the formal derivative rule. As an exact identity on $\mathcal{F}_b$:

$$
[a_m, (a_{m'}^\dagger)^n] = n \delta_{mm'} (a_{m'}^\dagger)^{n-1} \tag{8.14}
$$

$$
[(a_m)^n, a_{m'}^\dagger] = n \delta_{mm'} (a_m)^{n-1} \tag{8.15}
$$

*(Technical Note on the Regulator $K$: If these operators are evaluated on the capped space $\mathcal{F}_{\le K}$ using the regularized creator $\tilde{a}^\dagger$, this identity holds exactly on all states $X^r$ where $r + n \le K$. It breaks identically if the budget is exceeded, highlighting the finite Lie algebra boundary effect).*

**Lemma 8.8 (Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff Exactness).**
Because the commutator $[a_m, a_{m'}^\dagger]$ evaluates to a scalar multiple of the identity (a central element), the Lie algebra is nilpotent of step 2. Consequently, the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff (BCH) formula truncates exactly. For any scalars $\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{C}$:

$$
e^{\alpha a_m} e^{\beta a_m^\dagger} = e^{\beta a_m^\dagger} e^{\alpha a_m} e^{\alpha \beta I} \tag{8.16}
$$

**Definition 8.9 (Normal Ordering).**
The bosonic normal ordering operation, denoted by $:\mathcal{O}:$, is defined algebraically by physically transposing all annihilation operators $a_m$ to the right of all creation operators $a_m^\dagger$, without evaluating the commutators.

**Theorem 8.10 (Wick's Theorem for Bosons).**
Any product of linear bosonic operators can be written as a sum of normal-ordered products, summing over all possible pairwise contractions. For a single unnormalized position operator $\hat{x}_m := a_m + a_m^\dagger$, the expansion is:

$$
(\hat{x}_m)^n = (a_m + a_m^\dagger)^n = \sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2 \rfloor} \frac{n!}{k!(n-2k)! 2^k} :(\hat{x}_m)^{n-2k}: \tag{8.17}
$$

**Lemma 8.11 (Position Operator and Generating Functions).**
When the normal-ordered expansion of $\hat{x}_m^n$ is evaluated on the vacuum state $1 \in \mathcal{F}_b$, all right-acting annihilation operators evaluate to zero, leaving only pure creation operators.

$$
(\hat{x}_m)^n (1) = \sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2 \rfloor} \frac{n!}{k!(n-2k)! 2^k} X_m^{n-2k} \tag{8.18}
$$

This produces the *modified (unsigned) probabilist's Hermite polynomials*.
We can prove this elegantly using the generating function evaluated via exact BCH (Lemma 8.8):
$$ e^{t \hat{x}_m} (1) = e^{t(a_m^\dagger + a_m)} (1) = e^{t a_m^\dagger} e^{t a_m} e^{t^2/2} (1) = e^{t X_m + t^2/2} $$
Extracting the power series coefficient of $t^n / n!$ yields exactly the polynomial in Equation 8.18.

#### 8.7 Discussion: Truncation Errors, Projections, and Limits

Because we enforce the global hard cap $K$ via the filtration $\mathcal{F}_{\le K}$, we must carefully manage how operators interact with the boundaries of this finite-dimensional space.

##### 8.7.1 The Domain Shift Problem
While the annihilation operator $a_m$ strictly lowers the degree (mapping $\mathcal{F}_{\le K} \to \mathcal{F}_{\le K-m}$), the creation operator $a_m^\dagger$ raises it. Acting on a state at the top of the budget $\psi \in \mathcal{F}_{\le K}$ with $a_m^\dagger$ produces a state in $\mathcal{F}_{\le K+m}$. If we attempt to define $a_m^\dagger$ as an endomorphism strictly *on* $\mathcal{F}_{\le K}$, the raw operator fails to close.

##### 8.7.2 Regularization via Projection
To resolve the domain shift and close the algebra exactly on the finite-dimensional space, the most natural computational regularization is to introduce a strict projection operator $P_K : \mathcal{F}_b \to \mathcal{F}_{\le K}$. We then redefine the regularized creation operator as:

$$
\tilde{a}_m^\dagger := P_K \circ a_m^\dagger \tag{8.19}
$$

This ensures that $\tilde{a}_m^\dagger$ is a valid endomorphism on $\mathcal{F}_{\le K}$. Any excitation that exceeds the energy budget $K$ is immediately annihilated. This exactly mirrors the physical truncation behavior of the fermionic Dirac sea margins, providing a perfect algebraic analog to the finite lattice cutoff.

##### 8.7.3 The Infinite-Dimensional Limit Breakdown
Why not simply take the inductive limit $K \to \infty$ to recover standard continuum quantum mechanics? Mathematically, taking this limit breaks the algebraic rigidity we have established. By the Stone-von Neumann theorem, infinite-dimensional CCR representations suffer from unbounded domain issues. Furthermore, trace paradoxes arise: if $\mathcal{F}$ were infinite-dimensional, taking the trace of the exact CCR $[a, a^\dagger] = I$ yields $0 = \infty$. The energy budget $K$ prevents these analytical singularities.

##### 8.7.4 The Loss of Coherent States (and why it doesn't matter)
By choosing bounded polynomials, we intentionally sacrifice certain infinite-dimensional features. Most notably, an exact **coherent state** $|\alpha\rangle$ is defined as an eigenstate of the annihilation operator: $a |\alpha\rangle = \alpha |\alpha\rangle$.
Solving the differential equation $\frac{\partial}{\partial X} p(X) = \alpha p(X)$ yields the infinite formal power series $p(X) = C e^{\alpha X}$. This infinite series does *not* exist in the capped space $\mathcal{F}_{\le K}$.

If we attempt to approximate it with a truncated Taylor series $|\alpha\rangle_K = \sum_{n=0}^K \frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}} |n\rangle$, the eigenvalue equation fails by an exact error term at the boundary:
$$ a |\alpha\rangle_K = \alpha |\alpha\rangle_{K-1} = \alpha |\alpha\rangle_K - \alpha \frac{\alpha^K}{\sqrt{K!}} |K\rangle $$
**Is this a problem?** No. For the purposes of Phase 1 and Phase 2 bosonization (e.g., proving the Sugawara construction and Haldane completeness), we *never* require coherent states. We rely entirely on the exact Fock states (occupations and integer partitions), which are perfectly described by finite polynomials.

#### 8.8 Technical Notes for the Lean 4 Formalization (Chapter 8)

1. **Defining Normal Ordering in Lean:**
   * Do not attempt to define normal ordering as a syntactic macro (like `N[...]`).
   * Instead, define it rigorously as a computable `LinearMap`. In Lean, you define `normalOrder : MvPolynomial Q ℂ →ₗ[ℂ] Module.End ℂ (MvPolynomial Q ℂ)`. You specify its action on the basis monomials $X^r$ to evaluate to the linear endomorphism $(a^\dagger)^r a^s$ on the target space.

2. **Carrier Space as `MvPolynomial`:**
   * Define $\mathcal{F}_b$ as `MvPolynomial Q ℂ`. This automatically provides an extensive commutative algebra API and guarantees that repeated application of `pderiv` terminates at 0.

3. **Operators as `LinearMap`s:**
   * Define $a_m$ and $a_m^\dagger$ as terms of type `Module.End ℂ (MvPolynomial Q ℂ)`. Use `LinearMap.mulLeft` for creation and `pderiv` for annihilation.

4. **Weight Filtration and `totalDegree`:**
   * Mathlib's standard `totalDegree` computes the unweighted degree. For $\mathcal{F}_{\le K}$, you must define a custom `weightedDegree` function (or evaluate against a weighted sum map) that weights each variable $X_m$ by $m$, and then use `Submodule.span` over the basis.

5. **No-Limits Nilpotency Proof:**
   * Proving exact nilpotency is a matter of showing that `weightedDegree (pderiv m p) <= weightedDegree p - m`. Since $m \ge 1$, applying the derivative $K+1$ times strictly drops the degree below 0, yielding the zero polynomial identically.
