### Chapter 6: The Lattice AQFT Net

In algebraic quantum field theory (AQFT), the primary objects of study are not just states, but the local algebras of observables associated with specific spatial regions. On our finite lattice, this structure becomes a net of finite-dimensional operator algebras. The algebraic formalization strictly follows [Appendix A02](../appendices/a02_car_hilbert_and_normal_ordering.md).

**A Note on AQFT Axioms (No Assumptions Required):**
In this formalization, we assume absolutely no physical AQFT axioms. Because we explicitly construct the local algebras $\mathfrak{A}(I)$ from the fundamental position fermions acting on a strictly finite-dimensional Euclidean space, the traditional axioms are reduced to rigorous algebraic theorems proven directly from the CAR representation.

#### 6.1 Definitions

**Definition 6.1 (Local Algebras).**
For any spatial region $I \subseteq \Lambda$, the local algebra $\mathfrak{A}(I)$ is defined as the unital star-subalgebra of the endomorphism space $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{Fock}(\Lambda^*))$ generated by the local spatial operators within that region.

$$
\forall I \subseteq \Lambda, \quad \mathfrak{A}(I) := \mathrm{StarGen}_{\mathbb{C}}\{ c_x \mid x \in I \} \tag{6.1}
$$

Over the empty set, the algebra collapses to the scalar field multiples of the identity: $\mathfrak{A}(\emptyset) = \mathbb{C} \cdot I$.

**Definition 6.2 (Parity Automorphism and Grading).**
Using the global parity operator $\Gamma$ defined in Chapter 4, we define the parity automorphism $\alpha \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{A}(\Lambda))$ as:

$$
\forall A \in \mathfrak{A}(\Lambda), \quad \alpha(A) := \Gamma A \Gamma \tag{6.2}
$$

This induces a strict $\mathbb{Z}_2$-grading $\mathfrak{A}(I) = \mathfrak{A}_+(I) \oplus \mathfrak{A}_-(I)$.
The even part $\mathfrak{A}_+(I)$ is a strict subalgebra, whereas the odd part $\mathfrak{A}_-(I)$ is merely a linear subspace (since the product of two odd operators is even).

$$
\mathfrak{A}_\pm(I) := \{ A \in \mathfrak{A}(I) \mid \alpha(A) = \pm A \} \tag{6.3}
$$

---

#### 6.2 Lemmas and Theorems

**Lemma 6.3 (Isotony & Additivity).**
The local observable mapping respects spatial inclusions and unions. For all spatial subsets $I, J \subseteq \Lambda$:
1. **Isotony:** $I \subseteq J \implies \mathfrak{A}(I) \subseteq \mathfrak{A}(J)$.
2. **Additivity:** $\mathfrak{A}(I \cup J) = \mathfrak{A}(I) \vee \mathfrak{A}(J)$ (where $\vee$ represents the generated algebraic span).

**Lemma 6.4 (Twisted Locality).**
For any two disjoint spatial regions $I \cap J = \emptyset$, and for any homogeneous elements $A \in \mathfrak{A}_{\sigma_A}(I)$ and $B \in \mathfrak{A}_{\sigma_B}(J)$ with exact parities $\sigma_A, \sigma_B \in \{0, 1\}$:

$$
A B = (-1)^{\sigma_A \sigma_B} B A \tag{6.4}
$$

*Corollary:* The even subnet is strictly local, meaning $[A, B] = 0$ for all $A \in \mathfrak{A}_+(I)$ and $B \in \mathfrak{A}_+(J)$.

To prove this algebraically without topological limits, ordinary adjoin induction is insufficient because it cannot keep arbitrary partial sums homogeneous. Instead, we use a rigorous graded-word lemma over homogeneous polynomials of operators.

**Lemma 6.5 (Global Algebra Irreducibility).**
The global net evaluated over the entire lattice $\Lambda$ equals the full endomorphism algebra:

$$
\mathfrak{A}(\Lambda) = \mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{Fock}(\Lambda^*)) \tag{6.5}
$$

This is proven constructively by building diagonal occupation projectors and exact matrix units $|S\rangle\langle S'|$ from fixed ordered strings of creators and annihilators, carefully dividing out their nonzero algebraic signs.
