### Chapter 6: The Lattice AQFT Net

In algebraic quantum field theory (AQFT), the primary objects of study are not just states, but the local algebras of observables associated with specific spatial regions. On our finite lattice, this structure becomes a net of finite-dimensional operator algebras.

**A Note on AQFT Axioms (No Assumptions Required):**
In standard continuum AQFT, properties such as Isotony, Locality, and Additivity (the Haag-Kastler axioms) are postulated as the foundational axioms of the theory. **In this formalization, we assume absolutely no physical AQFT axioms.** Because we explicitly construct the local algebras $\mathfrak{A}(I)$ from the fundamental position fermions acting on a strictly finite-dimensional Hilbert space, the traditional AQFT axioms cease to be assumptions. Instead, they are reduced to **rigorous algebraic theorems** (see Lemmas 6.3 and 6.4) that are proven directly from the Canonical Anticommutation Relations (CAR) and standard matrix algebra.

#### 6.1 Definitions

**Definition 6.1 (Local Algebras).**
For any spatial region $I \subseteq \Lambda$, the local algebra $\mathfrak{A}(I)$ is defined as the unital $*$-subalgebra of the endomorphism space $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{Fock}(\Lambda^*))$ generated by the local spatial operators within that region.

$$
\forall I \subseteq \Lambda, \quad \mathfrak{A}(I) := \mathrm{Gen}_{\mathbb{C}}\{ c_x, c_x^\dagger \mid x \in I \} \tag{6.1}
$$

Over the empty set, the algebra collapses to the scalar field multiples of the identity: $\mathfrak{A}(\emptyset) = \mathbb{C} \cdot I$.

**Definition 6.2 (Parity Automorphism and Grading).**
Using the global parity operator $\Gamma := \prod_{k \in \Lambda^*} (I - 2n_k)$ defined in Chapter 4 (which equivalently evaluates to $(-1)^{\hat{N}_{\mathrm{tot}}}$), we define the parity automorphism $\alpha \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{A}(\Lambda))$ as:

$$
\forall A \in \mathfrak{A}(\Lambda), \quad \alpha(A) := \Gamma A \Gamma \tag{6.2}
$$

This induces a strict $\mathbb{Z}_2$-grading on every local algebra $\mathfrak{A}(I) = \mathfrak{A}_+(I) \oplus \mathfrak{A}_-(I)$, where the even (bosonic) and odd (fermionic) subcomponents are:

$$
\mathfrak{A}_\pm(I) := \{ A \in \mathfrak{A}(I) \mid \alpha(A) = \pm A \} \tag{6.3}
$$

#### 6.2 Lemmas and Theorems

**Lemma 6.3 (Isotony & Additivity).**
The local observable mapping respects spatial inclusions and unions. For all spatial subsets $I, J \subseteq \Lambda$:
1. **Isotony:** $I \subseteq J \implies \mathfrak{A}(I) \subseteq \mathfrak{A}(J)$.
2. **Additivity:** $\mathfrak{A}(I \cup J) = \mathfrak{A}(I) \vee \mathfrak{A}(J)$ (where $\vee$ represents the generated algebraic span of the union of the two subalgebras).

**Lemma 6.4 (Twisted Locality).**
For all $x \in \Lambda$, the fundamental generator satisfies $\alpha(c_x) = -c_x$. For any two disjoint spatial regions $I, J \subseteq \Lambda$ (such that $I \cap J = \emptyset$), and for any homogeneous elements $A \in \mathfrak{A}_{\sigma_A}(I)$ and $B \in \mathfrak{A}_{\sigma_B}(J)$ with exact parities $\sigma_A, \sigma_B \in \{0, 1\}$:

$$
A B = (-1)^{\sigma_A \sigma_B} B A \tag{6.4}
$$

*Corollary:* The even subnet is strictly local, meaning $[A, B] = 0$ for all $A \in \mathfrak{A}_+(I)$ and $B \in \mathfrak{A}_+(J)$.

**Lemma 6.5 (Global Algebra).**
The global net evaluated over the entire lattice $\Lambda$ is irreducible and equals the full endomorphism algebra:

$$
\mathfrak{A}(\Lambda) = \mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{Fock}(\Lambda^*)) \tag{6.5}
$$

Consequently, the complex dimension is exactly $\dim_{\mathbb{C}} \mathfrak{A}(\Lambda) = 4^L$.

#### 6.3 Technical Notes for the Lean 4 Formalization (Chapter 6)

1. **Subalgebras and `Algebra.adjoin`:**
   - In Lean, $\mathfrak{A}(I)$ should not be defined as a bare set, but using `StarSubalgebra` or `Algebra.adjoin ℂ` over the set of operators $\{c_x, c_x^\dagger \mid x \in I\}$.
   - Banning trivial implementations: The architect rule strictly forbids defining this as the bottom element $\bot$. `Algebra.adjoin` structurally guarantees a valid subalgebra.
2. **Proving Isotony and Additivity:**
   - These proofs will rely heavily on the monotonicity of Galois connections and the existing Mathlib API for `Algebra.adjoin`. `Isotony` maps directly to `adjoin_mono`.
3. **Induction for Twisted Locality:**
   - To prove $A B = (-1)^{\sigma_A \sigma_B} B A$, do not expand $A$ and $B$ into arbitrary matrix sums. Instead, use structural induction (specifically `adjoin_induction` or `StarSubalgebra.induction_on` in Lean).
   - Prove the relation first for the bare generators $c_x$ and $c_y^\dagger$ (using the Position CAR from Lemma 5.3), and then show that the relation is preserved under scalar multiplication, addition, and operator multiplication.
4. **Constructive Irreducibility (Lemma 6.5):**
   - The classical proof of Lemma 6.5 invokes Burnside's Theorem. In Lean, this is analytically heavy. Instead, use a **constructive matrix-unit proof**.
   - Show that the spatial generators can construct the momentum operators (via DFT). Then show that the momentum operators can construct diagonal projectors $P_S = \prod_{k \in S} n_k \prod_{p \notin S} (I - n_p)$. Finally, show that $c^\dagger_{S \setminus S'} c_{S' \setminus S} P_{S'}$ constructs the matrix unit $|S\rangle\langle S'|$. Since the matrix units span the entire $\mathrm{End}$ space, the algebra equals the entire space.
